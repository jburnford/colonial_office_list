Manufacturing and Industry.

Manufacturing is carried on somewhat extensively. The total capital employed in 1901 in factories having more than five hands was $441,053,060; the number of employés, 306,694; the wages paid $88,143,472 a year, and the annual product $452,775,577. The annual product of all mechanical industries, irrespective of number of hands employed, was $569,817,720, equal to 106·08 per head of the population.

Industry.

The main industry of the Dominion is agriculture, an enormous quantity of cereals and dairy produce being raised and exported. The fisheries of the maritime provinces are very extensive, and large quantities of dried, pickled, and canned fish and lobsters are exported. The lumber and fur trades are also important. Manufactures have been very considerably developed, the output being in the census year equal to $106·08 per head of the population. The output of wood pulp for the calendar year 1903 was valued at nearly five and a quarter million dollars. Coal and gold (in British Columbia, the Yukon Territory, and Nova Scotia) and copper are extensively worked. Valuable nickel (in Ontario) and phosphate mines exist. Shipbuilding is an important industry, and, besides ships, the principal among manufactured articles exported are furniture and other manufactures of wood, leather, agricultural and musical instruments. The principal imports are textile manufactures, coal, hardware, tea, sugar, raw cotton, hides, and tobacco.

The number of persons employed in connection with the fisheries is estimated at 90,000.

The arrivals and clearances were, in 1905, a total tonnage of 32,277,820, engaged in outside trade, and 44,377,261 tons engaged in coasting trade.

The number of steamers and sailing vessels on the Register Books of the Dominion on 31st December, 1905, was 7,325, with a net tonnage of 669,825. Of this number 2,654 were steamers, with a gross tonnage of 362,888. The number of seamen employed was about 128,563.

In 1905 the number of light stations was 822; of lighthouses, 1,038; of fog whistles, 44; and of automatic fog horns, 34.

Railways.

The length of steam railways in operation in the Dominion on 30th June, 1905, was 20,487 miles. Canada ranks eighth in absolute mileage among the states and kingdoms of the world possessing railway systems. The total capital invested amounted to $1,248,666,414 on 30th June, 1905. The total quantity of goods carried in the year 1905 amounted to 50,893,957 tons. The number of passengers was 25,288,723, and the distance travelled by trains was 65,934,114 miles. The total receipts were $106,467,199, and working expenses $79,977,574.

The Dominion Government owns and maintains 1,658 miles of railways, which have been constructed at a cost of $84,193,801. The working expenses in 1905 were $8,879,291, and the receipts, $7,000,853.
