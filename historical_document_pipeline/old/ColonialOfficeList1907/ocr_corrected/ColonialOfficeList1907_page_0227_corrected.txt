(2.) The Midland system starts from Port Elizabeth and joins the Western system at De Aar (339 miles). From Naauwpoort Junction (270 miles) the trunk line to the Orange River Colony and the Transvaal is continued.

The lines through the Orange River Colony (then Orange Free State) were built by the Cape Government Railways under convention with the Orange Free State, in terms of which the Cape constructed and worked the lines and the Orange Free State drew a moiety of the profits. On the 1st January, 1897, the Orange Free State Government took over these lines from the Cape and established its own railway department for the working of the lines in that territory.

In addition to the trunk line of the Midland system there is a line branching off at Zwart-kops, which touches Uitenhage and Graaff-Reinet and re-joins the main line at Rosmead Junction, after running a distance of 276 miles. This line passes through agricultural and fruit-growing country. From Klipplaat (123 miles from Port Elizabeth) on the Uitenhage-Graaff-Reinet line there is a branch to Oudtshoorn of 154 miles, through some of the most productive districts in the Colony.

Another branch line belonging to the Midland system is that from Alicedale to Graham’s Town (35 miles). This line is continued to Port Alfred (a seaside resort for the surrounding districts) by a railway owned by the Kowie Railway Company, Limited. This private branch is 43 miles in length, and was constructed under subsidy from the Government.

A junction line, 83 miles long, with the Eastern system (No. 3) at present exists from Rosmead Junction (243 miles from Port Elizabeth) to Stormberg Junction (326 miles from Port Elizabeth and 221 from East London); this line forms part of the Midland system. From a point—Bamboo Junction—on this line there is a branch about 17 miles in length, which serves the Cape Collieries and other companies’ coal mines with railway communication. By Act 38 of 1902 the Government authorised the expenditure of the sum of 30,000l. for the purpose of extending this line from its present terminus to Romansfontein, a distance of eight miles, but, up to the present (August, 1905), construction work on this Branch has not been commenced.

Parliament authorised (by Act 34, 1906,) the construction of a line, 24 miles long, from Schoombie to Maraisburg, at a cost of £100,000. There is also a branch, 2 miles in length, from Colesberg Junction, on the line from Naauwpoort to Norval’s Pont, to Colesberg Town.

A junction line further south, through agricultural and stock-producing districts having a large native population, runs from Somerset East to Cookhouse (on the main line of the Midland system), and on, via Fort Beaufort, to King William’s Town (on the Eastern system), thus forming another connection between the Midland and Eastern systems, and considerably shortening the journey by rail between Port Elizabeth and East London. The total length of this line is 152 miles.

In Act 29 of 1904 provision was made by Parliament for the construction of a line from the seaport of Mossel Bay, lying between Cape Town and Port Elizabeth, to George (33 miles), at a cost not exceeding 353,231l. Twenty-three miles of rails at the Mossel Bay end have already been laid. By Act 34 of 1906 Parliament authorised the extension of this railway from George to Oudtshoorn, a distance of 46½ miles, at a cost of £372,971, to join the branch which has already been constructed to the latter place from Klipplaat, on the Graaff Reinet line.

In 1898 Parliament authorised the construction of a narrow-gauge line from a point at or near Port Elizabeth to Avontuur, a distance of 179 miles. This line and the Hopefield line on the Western system are a new departure in Cape Government railway construction and working, as they are light railways of a 2 feet gauge instead of the standard gauge of 3 feet 6 inches. The experiment will be watched with much interest. The section of the line Port Elizabeth to Assegai Bosch, 102 miles, has been opened for traffic, and the whole line to Avontuur is expected to be opened early in 1907. The construction and working of a branch line (34 miles long) from a point on this railway near Port Elizabeth to Walmer, on a 2 feet gauge, has been authorised per Act 34 of 1906, on the understanding that the Walmer Municipality reimburses the Government the interest on the cost of construction and equipment of the line, and any loss on working, for a period of ten years.

Per Act 16 of 1904 Parliament authorised the construction by the South-Western Railway Company, Limited, of a 2 feet gauge line from Knysna to a point near Yzernek in the district of Knysna, a distance of about 16½ miles. In terms of this Act the Company is being granted a subsidy by the Government calculated at the rate of 800l. per mile. Construction work has been commenced on this line.

The Government, per Act 34, 1906, offers a subsidy of not exceeding £1,300 per mile, for the construction of a railway from a point on the South Western Railway to an approved point on the Mossel Bay-George Railway, either of 3 feet 6 inch or 2 feet gauge.

By the same Act Parliament has sanctioned the construction of a 2 feet gauge railway from Barkly Bridge (26½ miles from Port Elizabeth, on the main line), to a point on the Kowie Railway, between Roundhill and Port Alfred, via Alexandria, a distance of 90 miles, at a cost of £313,377.

(3) The Eastern system starting from East London was originally constructed to Aliwal North (280 miles) lying on the south bank of the Orange River, the boundary between the Cape Colony and the Orange River Colony.

In 1891 a junction with the trunk lines through the Orange Free State towards the Transvaal was effected by a connecting line from a point 33 miles south of Aliwal North to Springfontein, in the Orange Free State (314 miles from East London).

As before stated, through communication between the Eastern, Midland and Western districts exists, and a junction line further south has been constructed from Cookhouse to King William’s Town, thus forming another connection between the Midland and Eastern systems.

There is a branch line from Blaney to King William’s Town (10 miles), an old-established centre, and formerly a military station of some magnitude, and a branch line (66 miles) connects Sterkstroom with the Indwe Coal Mines.

There is also a light line of standard gauge between Bowker’s Park and Tarkastad, a distance of 33 miles.
