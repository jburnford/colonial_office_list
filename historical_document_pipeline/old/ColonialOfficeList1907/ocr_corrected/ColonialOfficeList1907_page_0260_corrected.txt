For purposes of general administration, the Island is divided into nine Provinces, presided over by Government Agents, who with their assistants and subordinate Headmen are the channel of communication between the Government and the people.

Defence.

Imperial troops are stationed at Colombo and Kandy, and an annual contribution is paid to the Imperial Government for the cost of the garrison. The Colonial contribution for 1905 was rs. 2,439,784. It is fixed by ordinance at 9½ per cent. of the general revenue (less land sales and railway charges), but is never to exceed three-fourths of the cost of the garrison. Colombo harbour has been strongly fortified at the joint expense of the Colonial and Imperial Governments.

The volunteer force of the Colony at the end of December, 1905, consisted of 2,479 of all ranks.

The number of Indian coolies on the tea estates, &c., is about 400,000. They are under no indentures, and are free to quit on giving a month’s notice. The total number of plantation labourers, including coolies born and settled in Ceylon, as well as of other races, is estimated at 440,000.

There are about 1,700 plumbago mines and pits in the island, which gave an aggregate yield of roughly 613,000 cwts. of mineral in 1905, the value of which may be estimated at over rs. 7,000,000. There are about 400 gem quarries.

The pearl banks near Manaar have been leased to an English Company for a term of twenty years from January 1st, 1906, at a rental of rs. 310,000 per annum, it being stipulated that a sum of between rs. 50,000 and rs. 150,000, at the discretion of the Government, shall be expended annually by the Company upon the improvement of the banks on scientific lines.

The minimum upset price of Crown land is rs. 15 per acre. 35,500 acres were sold in 1905 at an average price of rs. 36 per acre. A large demand has recently arisen for land suitable for rubber cultivation.

The revenue is principally derived from Customs Duties, Land Sales, Licences (under which head is entered the amount realised by the sale of Arrack Rents), Salt (which is a Government monopoly), Stamps, and Railway Receipts. The revenue from the sale of salt in 1905 was rs. 1,406,602. This is local salt worked by the Government, but the importation of foreign salt is allowed.

The local revenues raised by the Municipalities of Colombo, Kandy, and Galle, by the Provincial Road Committees, and by the Local Boards of Health and Improvement which have been established in the towns of Kalutara, Negombo, Minuwangoda, Gampola, Nuwara Eliya, Matale, Nawalapitiya, Hatton Dickoya, Matara, Batticaloa, Trincomalee, Kurunégala, Puttalam, Chilaw, Anuradapura, Badulla, Ratnapura, and Kegalla, amounted in 1905 to rs. 2,158,179.

The municipal debt on 31st December, 1905, was 380,381 rupees. Of this amount 164,706 rupees is the balance unpaid out of a loan of 250,000 rupees for the construction of waterworks at Kandy, and 55,763 rupees balance of a loan obtained by the municipality of Galle in 1890 for the construction of waterworks.
