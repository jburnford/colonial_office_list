MAIL AND STEAMSHIP SERVICES.

The British and African Steam Navigation Co. (1900), Ltd., and African Steamship Company.

Mail steamers of these companies leave Liverpool every Saturday calling at Sierra Leone, Monrovia, Axim, Sekondi, Cape Coast and Accra, the length of the voyage being 15 to 16 days.

These companies also despatch cargo steamers every alternate Wednesday and fourth Thursday from Liverpool, the Wednesday steamers calling at the principal Gold Coast ports and the Thursday steamers at all Gold Coast ports outwards and homewards. Length of the voyage, Liverpool to Accra, varies from 30 to 37 days.

On the 15th of each month a steamer leaves Hamburg and calls at the principal Gold Coast ports, length of voyage being about 30 days.

Homewards, the mail steamers of these companies call at Accra every Monday, also calling at Cape Coast, Sekondi and Axim. The length of the voyage, Accra to Plymouth, by these steamers is 17 days.

The steamers of these companies call at the Canary Islands or Madeira homewards.

Woermann Line.

A mail steamer of this line outwards from Hamburg calls at Southampton on 12th of each month for passengers for the principal Gold Coast ports, length of the voyage, Southampton to Accra, being 16 days. Cargo steamers of this line leave Hamburg on 1st, 7th, 20th and 22nd of each month, calling at most ports on the Gold Coast, the voyage to Accra varying from 25 to 32 days.

Two of this Company’s steamers leave Accra on 14th and 27th of each month homewards, calling also at Cape Coast, Sekondi and Axim for mails and passengers, the voyage, Accra to Southampton, taking about 16 days.

Cargo steamers of one or two French Companies sailing from Marseilles call irregularly outwards and homewards at Gold Coast ports.

Note: The above steamship services were in operation in October, 1906. In details they are liable to frequent changes.

Internal Communication.

The construction of a railway from Sekondi to Tarkwa, a distance of about 40 miles, was commenced in February, 1898. The rails reached Tarkwa on the 17th April, 1901, and in July of that year an extension was started from that point, the lines reaching Obuase (124 miles) by the end of 1902, and Kumase on 1st October, 1903. The receipts were 13,435l. in 1901, 39,438l. in 1902, 65,965l. in 1903, and 148,097l. in 1904, and 132,752l. in 1905.

The roads in the Colony that are maintained by the Department of Public Works are as follows:—

In the Axim district: Axim to the Ankobra River, 24 miles. In the Tarkwa district: Tarkwa to Cinnamon-Bippo, 9 miles. In the Cape Coast district: Cape Coast to Anomabo, 9½ miles; Cape Coast to Elmina, 8 miles; Cape Coast to Jukwa, 14½ miles; Cape Coast to Prahsu, 57½ miles.

In the Accra district: Accra to Kyebi, 59 miles; Accra to Aburi, 25½ miles; Junction on Aburi road to Dodowa, 13½ miles.

In addition to these there are numerous native roads, which are kept clear of bush and passable for traders and travellers by the local chiefs, who are paid by the Government for this purpose 10s. a quarter per mile. Of these the most worthy of mention are the road from Aburi through Apasare and Kofrodua to Osino, the Western frontier road, running through Aowin and Sefwi to Wam and British Gaman; the road from Cape Coast to Asafo and Debisu, vid Mampong; the roads from Saltpond, Appam and Winneba to Insuaim in Western Akyem; the roads from Aburi, through Akwapem and Kroko, to Akuse and Pong. A road has also been constructed from Accra to Kyebi by the Eastern Akyem Gold Fields Co., with assistance from the Government. Kumase is the centre for the various routes to the Northern District. From it roads lead to Gambaga, vid Kintampo, to Yeji and Prang, vid Atabubu, to Wam and British Gaman, vid Odumase and Berekum, and to Ahafo. It is also connected with the districts of the Colony by roads running into Sefwi and Akyem, as well as by the Cape Coast road mentioned above. Where rivers and swamps have to be crossed, ferries have been instituted, which are controlled for the most part by the Chiefs, who are empowered to levy a small fee. In some cases, where this arrangement has not given satisfaction, ferries are worked under direct Government supervision.

The principal waterway is the Volta River, which rises in the Kong Mountains, and flows into the sea at Adda, after a course of 200 miles. It is navigable for light draught launches as far as Akuse, and, with the exception of the Krakyi rapids, can be used for canoe traffic during certain seasons of the year as far as Yeji. As this route is the most direct to the Northern Territories, a transport service has been organised by the Government for this purpose. The Ankobra River has been largely used for transport purposes to the mines in the Prestea and Himan Districts, and is navigable for many months of the year by surf boats and light draught launches for a distance of 50 miles. The Tano, connected with Half Assini, by the main lagoon, and by a tramway, three miles in length, is navigable for light draught launches and canoes as far as Tano so, a distance of about 60 miles. Rapids at Attakwabo prevent this river being further utilised.

There is no harbour along the whole coast of the Colony, and passengers and goods are landed in surf-boats. A breakwater at Accra is, however, in course of construction.

The best landings are at Dixcove, Axim and Sekondi, while the surf is most dangerous at Adda, Saltpond, and the Apollonia Coast.

A Transport Department has been working for three years at Sekondi and Cape Coast, under the direction of the Government. Arrangements can be made for the conveyance of goods and mining material to any part of the Colony by this Department at a fixed tariff.

Postal Communication.

The Colony is included in the Postal Union.
