Telegraphs and Telephones.
Telegraphs were first introduced in 1882, when a line 24 miles in length was constructed between Accra and Christiansborg. This was in 1885 extended to Aburi, distant 25 miles. A line was next constructed along the coast from Accra to Axim, a distance of 180 miles, with intermediate stations at Winneba, Appam, Saltpond, Anamaboe, Cape Coast, Elmina, Chama, Sekondi, Adjua, and Dixcove. This has been duplicated, and in 1902 the system was extended from Axim to the French boundary at Newtown. Another line of about 114 miles in length extends from Accra to the Anglo-German frontier, where it joins the line of the German settlement of Togoland, with intermediate stations at Pram Pram, Ada, Kwitta, and Danoe. A third line strikes inland from Ada, and connects the towns of Akuse and Kpong, on the River Volta. A fourth has been constructed through the interior, starting at Cape Coast, and reaching as far as Bole, with intermediate stations at Mansu, Prahsu, Kumase, Nkoranza, Kintampo, and another branches off at Nkoranza, connecting Attabubu and Yeji. A fifth line runs along the railway from Sekondi to Kumase, with intermediate offices at Tarkwa, Dunkwa, and Obuasi. A line has been constructed connecting Accra with Kpong, vid Aburi and Akropong. From this there is a branch line to Dodowa. The total length of lines constructed is, so far, 1,078 miles.

In 1893 the number of telegrams forwarded was 60,031; in 1895, 81,690; in 1897, 84,580; in 1899, 128,026; in 1900, 140,315; in 1901, 199,424; in 1902, 248,077; in 1903, 276,681; in 1904, 270,518; and in 1905, 193,877.

Accra and Sekondi are in telegraphic communication with Europe—vid Sierra Leone, St. Vincent, and Madeira—with Lagos, Brass, Bonny, Cameroons, and St. Thomas. They are also connected with the Cape, vid Loanda and Mossamedes, and vid Sierra Leone, Ascension and St. Helena.

Defence Force.
A military force was raised by Captain Glover, R.N. (afterwards Sir John Glover), and Mr. (afterwards Sir Roger) Goldsworthy for service in the Ashanti War 1873-4, and was known as Glover’s Hausas. The force was converted into Constabulary in 1879, and into a Regiment of Infantry and three Batteries of Artillery in 1901. The present establishment is one Battalion of Infantry, with one Company in the Northern Territories, and one Battery of Artillery, and forms part of the West African Frontier Force.

Gold Coast Volunteers.
The Gold Coast Volunteer Force consists of four Battalions, viz.:—
1. Gold Coast Volunteer Corps.
2. Gold Coast Railway Volunteers.
3. Gold Coast Mines Volunteers.
4. Ashanti Mines Volunteers.

The Gold Coast Volunteer Corps was originally formed in 1892, and is at present composed of five companies, two of which, together with the headquarters, are stationed at Accra, one at Cape Coast, one at Sekondi, and one at Axim. The Gold Coast Railway Volunteers were formed in 1905 for the purpose of defending the railway. It consists of four companies, three at Sekondi and one at Obuasi. They are composed of railway officials.
