NATAL.

A line runs from the South Coast Branch at Alexandra Junction to Umzinto, 6¾ miles distant, and serves an important sugar-growing area.

A further branch connecting the main line with the Bluff side (Wests) goes out from Clairmont on the South Coast Line, and is 6¾ miles in length.

A branch line runs from Maritzburg (the Natal Trunk Line junction) to Donnybrook, a distance of 79¾ miles, and a further section to Creighton (95 miles from Maritzburg), is in course of construction. It is then proposed to continue the line to Riverside on the Griqualand East boundary, and when completed will form a new connection with Cape Colony.

A 2 feet light railway is being constructed between Esperanza, on the Umzinto branch, to Donnybrook, on the Natal-Cape line, passing through Stuartstown, and when completed will be about 100 miles in length.

Another 2 feet light railway is also being built between Estcourt, on the main line, and Weenen, a distance of 30 miles.

Starting from Ennersdale (on the main line), a branch line is being built to Los Kop, 13½ miles, and when this section has been completed it is proposed to extend the line to Springfield, a further distance of 10½ miles.

The work of railway construction was begun in January, 1876, and the sections then authorised were completed in December, 1880. Two subsequent extensions of 119 and 114 miles respectively were sanctioned, and the last, which completes the main line to Charlestown on the border of the Transvaal, was opened for passenger traffic on 7th April, 1891, and for goods traffic on 1st August, 1891. Connection with Johannesburg and Pretoria was opened on the 1st December, 1895.

The total cost of construction of the railways up to the end of 1905 was 12,957,544l. The receipts for 1905 were 2,052,487l. 12s. 2d., and the expenditure 1,307,010l. 7s. 3d. The net receipts were equal to 5l. 15s. 0·78d. per cent. upon the capital expended on open lines.

In addition to the railways owned and worked by the Natal Government there are two private lines in Natal. The Tea Estates of Messrs. J. L. Hulett and Sons, Ltd., are served by a 2 feet narrow gauge railway which branches off from Stanger Station, and is 8 miles in length. The ruling gradient of the railway is 1 in 30.

The Lower Illovo Sugar Estate, situated on the south coast about 22 miles from Durban, possesses a 2 feet narrow gauge line 8 miles in length. The grades vary from 1 to 4·81 in 100.

Defence.

There was, in 1905, an Imperial garrison quartered at Pietermaritzburg, consisting of one infantry battalion, besides detachments of the usual departmental corps. The total strength of the Imperial Forces in Natal, 1905, was about 700, including details, of all ranks.

The volunteer force of the Colony has been converted into a militia, is partially paid, and is divided into a naval corps of 150 men, about 1,900 mounted riflemen, 4 field batteries of 340, and 2 infantry battalions of 827 men, besides departmental corps numbering 128, and a staff of 45. The whole force is fully equipped and armed with modern weapons.

The Colony also maintains an armed and mounted police force, numbering 870 Europeans, fully equipped and available for field service. The volunteers and police were on active service for over 12 months during the Boer War (1899–1902). Since the beginning of 1903 volunteering received a great impetus, owing to the passing of the Militia Act; and in September of that year the total number of all ranks was about 7,200.

Military training is compulsory on all boys over 10 years attending the Government schools. They are organised in cadet corps, and numbered 2,001 at end of 1905. There is also a senior cadet corps, formed during 1904, composed of youths who have left school of from 16 to 20 years of age, and numbering some 331. In addition to this, members of rifle associations are liable to be called out for defence purposes. At the end of 1905 there were 89 Rifle Associations, with a total strength, excluding Natal police and militiamen, of 3,004.

The Port of Durban is defended by batteries armed with modern heavy guns.

Industry.

The chief industries are the production of sugar, tea, wattle bark, wool, maize, potatoes, Kaffir corn and the mining of coal; dairying; fruit culture; and the breeding of cattle, sheep, pigs and poultry. The chief exports are coal, wool, mohair, wattle bark, tea, sugar, tobacco, maize, fruit and fruit preserves, hides and skins. The chief imports are apparel and cotton goods, haberdashery, woollen manufactures, leather goods, hardware and machinery, wooden manufactures, living animals, corn and grain, agricultural implements and seeds, articles of food and drink including frozen meats, tobacco, and books and stationery. A large and increasing trade, principally with the Orange River and Transvaal Colonies, is carried on, and the bulk of imports goes to those colonies. In 1896 the value of exports was 1,785,375l.; in 1905 their value had increased to 7,946,194l. In 1896 the value of imports was 5,437,862l.; and in 1905 they had increased to 10,721,475l.

The bar which used to exist at the entrance to the Port of Durban has been practically removed, by an extension of the permanent works and by constant dredging, the average depth at low water in 1905 being 28 ft. 10 ins., as against 12 ft. 1 in. in 1895. Large ships are now able at all times to come in and go out with perfect safety and discharge their cargoes directly on to the wharves.

Durban is a port of registry, having, in 1905, 27 vessels, with a tonnage of 1,740 tons net, including a number of dredgers owned by the Government, but excluding tugs.

The land under cultivation amounted in 1905 to 915,869 acres, of which 395,918 acres were cultivated by Europeans, and there is a good scope for dairying and stock-farming. The coal-mining industry continues to increase every year, the output for 1905 being 1,129,407 tons (the value of which at the pit’s mouth was 467,162l.), as against 216,106 tons in 1896 (value 108,053l. at the pit’s mouth). The amount consumed in the Railway Department in 1905 was 208,223 tons (value 112,906l.) There is also a considerable and growing trade in bunker and export coal, the coal bunkered in 1905 being 561,250 tons, and exported, 215,445 tons. There were 20 outputting collieries in 1905. The present output is at the rate of about 1,000,000 tons per annum. The freight charge for railway transport is ¾d. a ton per mile.
