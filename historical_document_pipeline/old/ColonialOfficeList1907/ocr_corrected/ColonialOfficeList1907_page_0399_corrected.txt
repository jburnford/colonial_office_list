Public Works.

The erection of public buildings and execution
of other public works has, during the past year,
received a very considerable impetus. The Loan
Fund of 500,000l. which was granted to the Colony
for new buildings, has in a great measure been
expended, the total number of buildings under-
taken being as follows:—

16 public offices.

18 post offices.

26 schools (permanent buildings, besides a very
large number of temporary schools for educa-
tional facilities in remote districts).

24 R.M.’s, schoolmasters’ and other official
residences.

25 police and gaol buildings.

11 bridges, and other buildings of a miscellaneous
nature.

Under the revenue of the Colony a further large
number of buildings have been carried on, and
requirements in all towns have been very fully
dealt with by the provision of thoroughly up-to-
date accommodation for the requirements of all
departments.

The extensions to the central offices in Bloem-
fontein have been completed, and arrangements
are being made for large public works in the erection
of a new Central College and University for the
Colony, as well as new Law Courts and a National
Museum. The expenditure on these important
works will probably amount to 200,000l.

Education.

The educational policy of the Government is
administered through the Education Department.
From 1900 to 1905 the schools were managed,
teachers were selected and appointed, and all
expenditure was borne by the Government. Local
Education Committees were established in the
towns in 1903, but these were merely advisory
bodies. In terms of an agreement come to
between the Government and representatives of
the Dutch Reformed Church in March, 1905, the
Christian National Schools, which had been
started a year previously, were amalgamated with
the Government Schools and a new Ordinance
introducing certain administrative changes was
passed.

Under the provisions of this Ordinance the
areas of jurisdiction of School Committees have
been enlarged, and the bare majority of the
members have been elected by the ratepayers.
The powers of these Committees have also been
extended in return for the local financial responsi-
bility provided for in the Ordinance.

At the end of June, 1904, the number of pupils
attending the Government Schools of the Colony
was 12,507. In June, 1905, the attendance had
increased to 15,577, and in June, 1906, the number
on the roll was 16,338, or approximately twice
the highest number reached under the former
Government. Education in all schools, with the
exception of High Schools, is free and open to all
children of full European parentage, and the
Education Ordinance of 1905 provides for com-
pulsory attendance, subject to certain conditions.

Students are prepared for the B.A. and M.A.
Degrees, Law, Surveying and Engineering Ex-
aminations at Grey University College in Bloem-
fontein, new buildings for which are now in the
course of erection on an extensive site in the west
of the city.

A Normal School in Bloemfontein provides for
the training of teachers, and bursaries are given
on the results of an entrance examination held
annually in April.

In accordance with an agreement arrived at
in June, 1905, between the Government and the
leading religious denominations in the Colony,
religious instruction of a purely historical character
is given in all Government Schools for two hours
every week. Facilities are also afforded to the
Ministers of the various denominations to give
instruction to the children of their own congre-
gations immediately after school hours, for one
hour on one day in each week, in cases where the
parents make a special request for such instruction.
