SWAZILAND.

Swaziland lies to the East of the Transvaal and is bounded on the East by Portuguese territory and Tongaland, now part of Natal. Its extent is 6,536 square miles, and its population consists of 85,491 natives and 890 whites. The natives are of a kindred race to the Zulus. Both the Boers and Cetewayo claimed authority over it. Her Majesty’s Government, however, in the Conventions of Pretoria (1881) and London (1884), expressly stipulated for the independence of Swaziland.

The Swazis made little good use of their independence. The King Umbandine granted away to concessionaires practically all sources of revenue, lands, minerals and every conceivable right which could be made a source of profit. It became necessary, in consequence of the number of white men in the country, that the Administration should be placed in more capable hands, and under a Convention of 1890 between Her Majesty and the South African Republic, a provisional Government was, with the consent of the Swazis, set up, consisting of a Government Committee selected by these two Powers and the Swazi Government, of a Government Secretary, and of a Chief Court. In accordance with pledges given to the Government of the South African Republic this administration came to an end three years later, when the British Government signed a fresh Convention, which permitted the South African Republic to acquire from the Queen Regent and her Council an Organic Proclamation (a Native Order in Council) conferring on the South African Republic rights of jurisdiction, legislation and administration without incorporation in the South African Republic. The Swazis never signed the Organic Proclamation which had been drafted for them, and accordingly its provisions with some of those in the 1893 Convention were embodied in the final Convention of 1894 under which the South African Republic exercised powers of protection, legislation, jurisdiction and administration in Swaziland, subject to the limitations of the Convention.

On the conquest of the Transvaal, all the rights and powers of the late South African Republic passed to His Majesty, but since the other party to the Convention of 1894 had disappeared without the limitations therein, an Order in Council under the Foreign Jurisdiction Act was issued in June, 1903, providing that the Governor of the Transvaal should administer Swaziland, and conferring on him the right to legislate by Proclamation.

In accordance with the Powers granted to him, the Governor issued a Proclamation in 1904 providing for Native Administration and for dealing with the concessions question. By it criminal jurisdiction was vested solely in the Resident magistrate, and an appeal was permitted from the decisions of the Chiefs in civil cases to his Court. The chiefs were otherwise confirmed in their civil jurisdiction as far as aboriginal natives were concerned.

The Governor provided for the appointment of a commission to deal with concessions. As already stated, the late King had granted, with the assent of his Council, all sorts of concessions, e.g., exemption from taxes, exclusive rights to import arms, to trade, to manufacture, rights over lands and minerals. These were all to be examined by the Commission. The Government was to have the right to expropriate the holders of any of the concessions, except those connected with land and minerals, on payment of the value of the concession before the war, provision being made that the Commission should decide the value in any case of dispute. The Commission reported early in 1906. During 1905 and 1906 the same Commission has been engaged in delimiting the boundaries of the concessions which give rights over land and minerals, with a view, not only of settling disputes, but also of providing for the natives. Lord Selborne also paid a visit to Swaziland in September, 1906, in order to discuss with the Queen Regent and her councillors various outstanding questions connected with the country.

Meanwhile, His Majesty’s Government having determined to transfer Swaziland to the control of the High Commissioner for South Africa on the grant of Responsible Government to the Transvaal, an Order in Council was issued on the 1st of December, 1906, making provision to this effect.
