SOUTHERN NIGERIA.

Situation and Boundaries.

The Colony and Protectorate of Southern
Nigeria comprise that portion of territory on the
West Coast of Africa which is bounded on the
south by the Atlantic Ocean, and on the west
by the line of the frontier of the French territory
of Dahomey, on the north and north-east by the
British Protectorate of Northern Nigeria and on
the east by the frontier of the German territory
of Kamerun. Its area is estimated at 77,260
square miles.

Constitution.

By Letters Patent dated 28th of February, 1906,
the Colony of Southern Nigeria was formed, and
was amalgamated with the Colony of Lagos and
its Protectorate under one Administration, under
the style of the “Colony and Protectorate of
Southern Nigeria,” with headquarters in Lagos
Town.

The Government is administered by a Governor
assisted by an Executive and Legislative Council
composed of official and unofficial members.

Administration.

For purposes of administration Southern
Nigeria is divided into three Provinces, namely:
the Western or Lagos Province comprising the
former Colony of Lagos and its Protectorate,
with headquarters in Lagos Town; the Central
or Niger Province with headquarters at Warri;
and the Eastern or Calabar Province with head-
quarters at Calabar; the two latter Provinces
comprising what was formerly known as the
Protectorate of Southern Nigeria. Each Province
is presided over by a Provincial Commissioner
and divided into Districts under the charge of
District Commissioners and Assistant District
Commissioners.

Foreign Money Order and Parcels Post systems
are in operation with the United Kingdom,
Northern Nigeria, Gold Coast, Sierra Leone and
Gambia, and orders are issued for sums up to 40l.
at a commission of 4d. per 1l. There is also
an Inland Money Order system, and insurance of
letters up to a maximum of 60l. can be effected.

An Inland Telegraph system is maintained by a
Calabar–Bonny Section consisting of 85 miles
land line and 31½ miles cable. In addition to this
an extensive system of telegraphs was initiated at
the beginning of 1905, and by the middle of 1906
direct telegraphic communication had been
inaugurated between the Central and Western
Provinces. The new system, when completed, will
render telegraphic communication possible between
the chief centres of the whole Colony and
Protectorate without having recourse to the
cable which at present runs from Lagos to Brass
and Bonny in the Eastern Province. At the
beginning of the last quarter of 1906 there were 24
telegraphic stations established in the Colony and
Protectorate in addition to those along the
Government Railway line from Lagos to Ibadan.
The principal telegraph stations in the Central
and Eastern Provinces are as follows:—Agbor,
Asaba, Benin City, Forcados, Ifon, Onitsha,
Oguta, Sapele, Warri, Eguanga, Bonny and
Calabar, the rate being 1d. per word with a
minimum charge of 1s.

Education.

The headquarters of the Director of Education
are in Lagos Town. For the Western Province
Government inspection and examinations are
maintained under the supervision of a Provincial
Inspector of Schools and a Board of Education.
There are well conducted Mission Schools con-
nected with the Church Missionary Society, and
the Roman Catholic and Wesleyan bodies at
Lagos, Abeokuta, Ibadan, Oyo and other places.
There is also a Municipal School at Ibadan.
There are about 4,400 pupils on the school rolls of
the various Christian denominations, and the
Government grants to Assisted Schools during the
year 1905 amounted to 2,204l. The Moham-
medans maintain their own schools, where
Arabic is taught. Six Government Schools in
which elementary instruction in English is given,
have been established for this section of the
population. Education is not compulsory.

The system of education provides for Primary
and Intermediate Schools, High Schools, and
Technical Schools. These are of two kinds:—
Government, and Assisted Schools; to the latter
are awarded grants-in-aid which are earned by
examination. The management and supervision
of district schools are vested in local committees
of management subject to the directions of
a Board which meets at Calabar.

In the Eastern Province there are 9 Government
Schools, the largest of which is at Bonny. It is
residential and provided with a Technical and
Industrial Department where carpentering,
coopering and other crafts are taught.

In the Central Province there are 17 Govern-
ment Schools. The most important of these are
at Benin City, Sapele, Onitsha and Warri.

There is a High School at Calabar under the
management of the United Presbyterian Free
Church of Scotland, styled the Hope Waddell
Training Institute. This school is financed by
Government in part and under certain conditions.
The Roman Catholic Mission has extended its
sphere of work on the Niger, and has erected
boys’ and girls’ schools at Calabar. In addition
to the above a number of schools are conducted
by the Church Missionary Society, the Niger
Delta Pastorate Mission, the Primitive Methodist
Mission and the Onitsha Industrial Mission.

There are 24 Mission Schools on the list of
Government Assisted Schools in the Central, and
19 in the Eastern Province.

Defence.

The Military Force consists of the Southern
Nigeria Regiment of the West African Frontier
Force, with an establishment of two battalions
and Artillery and Headquarter Staff, the total
strength of the Regiment being 63 Officers,
34 British Non-commissioned Officers, and 1,883
rank and file. The Civil Police Force maintained
numbers 980, including nine European officers.
