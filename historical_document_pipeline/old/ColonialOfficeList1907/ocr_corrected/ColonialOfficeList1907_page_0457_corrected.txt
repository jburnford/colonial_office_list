Railways.
The length of open line is 429 miles, as follows:—
Province Wellesley, Pérak, Sélangor, Negri Sembilan, and Malacca, 23, 190, 113, 82, and 21 miles respectively. There are also 21 miles in the Negri Sembilan worked by a company. The length of line under construction is 120 miles in the state of Johore. The total length of line surveyed is 200 miles, 181 miles in Pahang and 19 miles in Pérak. The length under survey is 139 miles in Pahang. Through communication between Prai in Province Wellesley to Gemas in the Negri Sembilan, a distance of 352 miles, was established on 1st October, 1906.

The total length of line from Prai to Singapore, when completed, excluding branches, will be about 500 miles. The branch lines run to Port Weld, Teluk Anson, Batu Caves, Port Swettenham and Port Dickson.

There is also a steam ferry service between Penang and Prai, the northern terminus of the system in Province Wellesley, a distance of 5 miles.

Pérak.
H.H. Sir Idris Mersid-el-A’zam Shah, G.C.M.G., Sultan.

Pérak is the most northerly of the Protected States on the west coast, touching Province Wellesley on the north and Sélangor on the south. It is one of the oldest States in the Peninsula, and, though subject to Achin at one time, and overrun by Kedah in 1821, it generally maintained its independence. About 1850 the discovery of tin in Larut was followed by the immigration of Chinese in great numbers. Since the appointment of a British Resident in 1874, the record of the State has been one of remarkable progress, interrupted only by the murder of the first British Resident, Mr. J. W. Birch, in 1875, the military occupation of the country, and the banishment to the Seychelles of the ex-Sultan and some chiefs implicated in the affair.

The State, which has an area of 6,550 square miles, is well watered, the Pérak, with its tributaries the Kinta, Batang Padang, Bidor and Sungkai, being the most important rivers. Other rivers of importance are the Krian, the Kurau and the Bernam, with its tributary, the Slim. The mountains on the east boundary, which are part of the main range of the Peninsula, rise to a height of over 7,000 feet.

The chief export is tin. Sugar and rice are extensively grown. The cultivation of rubber is now attracting great attention, and such consignments as have been exported have realised very high prices. In the Krian district the Government has completed an extensive scheme for the irrigation of more than 51,000 acres of rice land, the larger proportion of which is already under cultivation.

Larut, Kinta and Batang Padang are the chief mining districts. The district of Batang Padang lies between the Kinta valley and the Sélangor boundary, and contains rich deposits of tin, a little gold and wolfram.

The total mileage of completed roads in Pérak is 661 miles of metalled cart roads, and 627 miles of bridle and other paths.

The whole length of the State is traversed by the main line of railway from Penang.

The capital is Taiping, in the Larut district, where the British Resident resides.

The chief towns are Ipoh, Kuala Kangsar, Kampar, Tapah and Batu Gajah, and the two ports are Teluk Anson and Port Weld. His Highness the Sultan of Pérak resides at Kuala Kangsar.

Sélangor.
H.H. Suleiman bin Almerhum Raja Musa, C.M.G., Sultan.

Sélangor, situated on the Western side of the Peninsula, is bounded on the North by Pérak, on the East and South-east by Pahang and Negri Sembilan, and on the West by the Straits of Malacca.

The total area is estimated at 3,200 square miles, with a coast line extending for about 125 miles. Where the State touches the central range of the Peninsula there are several peaks of over 5,000 feet and one of 5,812 feet.

The State is drained by four rivers: The Bernam, forming the boundary between Pérak and Sélangor, the Sélangor, the Klang and the Langat. The inhabitants are said to be the descendants of a Colony from the Celebes.
