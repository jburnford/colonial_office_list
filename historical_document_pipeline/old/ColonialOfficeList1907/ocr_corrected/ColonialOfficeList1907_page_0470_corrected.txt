It was understood that the proceeds of this loan would be used for the extinction of liabilities, the purchase of public works and railways, and other local requirements. It was further understood that a further loan of 30,000,000l., bearing interest at 4%, should be issued, and that the proceeds of this loan should be paid to the Imperial Government as a contribution towards the cost of the war. This issue was to take place in three instalments of 10,000,000l., at an interval of at least twelve months between each, and the first instalment was to be raised about January, 1904, the success of this issue being guaranteed by certain South African firms. The loan was to be a charge on the general revenue of the Transvaal.

The necessary legislation having been passed in the Imperial Parliament and the local Legislature, the first of these loans, to the extent of 30,000,000l., was issued (at par) in May, 1903. Throughout this year, however, there was considerable depression in the Colony, and, as this state of things continued, His Majesty’s Government have not pressed for the raising of the second or war loan.

The balance of the 35,000,000l. loan (5,000,000l.) was raised about the middle of 1904. This loan was put up to tender, and realised an average price of 98l. 10s. 3d. By that time the whole of the first issue had been practically expended, counting in the amounts ear-marked for definite impending liabilities—such as the redemption of the shares of the Netherlands Railway Company. The heavy task of repairing the ravages of war and relieving the necessities of all classes of people in the new Colonies was dealt with from four funds, viz.:—

1. The 3,000,000l. mentioned in the Terms of Surrender, and paid as a free grant from Imperial funds.

2. 5,000,000l. appropriated for “repatriation and compensation” from the guaranteed loan. Both of these funds were fully spent by the middle of 1904, and a debit of about 600,000l. incurred; the expenditure had, however, almost ceased, and it is anticipated that the debit will eventually be extinguished by the repayment for supplies made in excess of the 3,000,000l.

3. 2,000,000l. allotted to compensation to “British subjects, foreign subjects, and natives.” This, like (1), was a free grant from Imperial funds. More than half of it was paid by the 30th April, 1904, to persons who had suffered losses from the war, at the rate of 13s. 4d. in the pound on their assessed losses, and the final instalment of 4s. in the pound brought the rate up to 17s. 4d., a very remarkable degree of compensation. 300,000l. was set aside for the compensation of natives.

4. 2,000,000l. paid by the War Office for disbursement to the “protected burghers,” being men to whom the military authorities had incurred some special obligation.

To this was added a further sum of 2,500,000l. to discharge the military receipts given during the war. This must be distinguished from the previous funds, as it was required for the payment of debts which formed part of the expenses of the war; the payments, however, in common with those under the previous heads, assisted the people to re-build, re-stock, and make a fresh start in their occupations. The amount actually paid has slightly exceeded the 2,500,000l.

Thus the total amount provided for relief was 14,500,000l., of which 2,500,000l. was in payment for goods received.

When the guaranteed loan was about to be issued, several difficulties arose as to the allocation of the proceeds, as there were various matters in which both of the new Colonies were interested, and it was accordingly decided to treat the railways as the joint property of the two Colonies, and to establish a common fund for that and the like common services. For these purposes the Inter-Colonial Council was constituted. (See under “South Africa.”) The revenue of this body for the year 1905-6 was 6,737,653l., which included railway receipts of 4,976,993l., and contributions from the Transvaal and Orange River Colony of 973,838l., the arrangement being that these contributions are (unless the Secretary of State decides otherwise) to be in proportion to their respective customs receipts for the preceding year. The expenditure was 6,752,629l., the principal items being:—Service of guaranteed loan, 687,710l.; South African Constabulary (about 5,000 men), 1,019,834l.; Central South African Railways, 4,940,465l.
