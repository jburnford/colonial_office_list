AUSTRALIA—NEW SOUTH WALES.

The coal mines have steadily advanced since 1863. The produce of 18 mines in that year is given as 433,889 tons, valued at 236,230l., while in 1881, 1,769,597 tons, valued at 603,248l., were raised from 40 mines; and, in 1905, 6,632,138 tons, valued at 2,903,461l., were raised from 104 mines.

Sydney and Newcastle are ports of registry; 48 vessels, of a total tonnage of 4,121, were registered during the year 1905, while the total number of vessels on the registers at the close of 1905 was 1,103, with a tonnage of 118,176.

Currency and Banking.

The currency is exclusively British sterling. At Sydney there is a branch of the Royal Mint at which gold was received during 1905 to the extent of 3,513,570l.

The 13 banks operating in the State in 1906 were:—Bank of New South Wales; Commercial Banking Company of Sydney, Ltd.; Bank of Australasia; Union Bank of Australia, Ltd.; Australian Joint Stock Bank, Ltd.; London Bank of Australia, Ltd.; English, Scottish, and Australian Bank, Ltd.; City Bank of Sydney; Queensland National Bank, Ltd.; Bank of North Queensland, Ltd.; Bank of New Zealand; Commercial Bank of Australia, Ltd.; and the National Bank of Australasia, Ltd. The average note circulation for the same period was 1,461,640l.

The New South Wales Savings Bank, established 1832, had, on the 1st January, 1906, 101,383 depositors, with 5,545,367l. deposited. Interest is allowed at the rate of 3½ per cent. per annum on deposits up to 200l. on accounts closed during the year, and 4 per cent. on accounts remaining open. When deposits made by any one individual exceed the sum of 200l. no interest is allowed on such excess; but this regulation, as in the case of the Post Office Savings Banks, does not apply to the deposits of Penny Savings Banks, Charitable Institutions, and Friendly Societies. The Government (Post Office) Savings Banks, established in 1871, had, on 30th June, 1906, 7,952,885l. deposited by 254,331 depositors. Interest is allowed at the rate of 3½ per cent. per annum on all deposits up to 300l.

Means of Communication.

There are 56,316 miles of roads (including 7,744 miles of municipal roads and streets). In addition, there are 3,508 bridges of 20 feet span and over, covering a length of 330,420 feet; 41,929 culverts = 581,865 feet. There are also 119 punts, 2 horse boats, 2 steam launches, 80 flood boats, 129 other boats belonging to the Government used for ferries. There are 3,390 miles of railways, owned and worked by the Government, the total cost of construction having been 43,626,063l. The receipts during the year ended 30th June, 1906, were 4,234,791l., and the working expenses 2,308,384l., the balance therefore available towards meeting the annual charge for interest being 1,926,407l. There are also 814 miles of private railway, the capital expenditure on which amounted to 553,389l. There are 126 miles of Government tramways, earning 851,488l., against working expenses amounting to 665,083l. The total capital expenditure on tramways was 3,669,096l.

Of telegraphs there are 14,827 miles open, with 71,086 miles of wire, with 1,069 stations. The cost of construction was 1,434,017l., and the revenue received during 1905, 150,830l.

Of Inland rivers the Murray, Darling, Murrumbidgee, and Lachlan are navigable most of the year, and nearly all the coast rivers are navigable, especially the Richmond, the Clarence, the Macleay, the Manning, the Hunter, the Hawkesbury, and the Shoalhaven, while Port Jackson, Port Stephens and Jervis Bay are unrivalled natural harbours. Steamers leave Sydney regularly for nearly all parts of the world, but the direct communication with Europe is chiefly by the vessels of the P. and O. (vid Colombo and Brindisi), the Orient Royal Mail lines (vid Suez and Naples), and by those of the Messageries Maritimes (vid Marseilles), Oceanic Company (vid San Francisco), Canadian Australian line (vid Vancouver), North German Lloyd (vid Genoa), White Star line (vid Durban and Capetown), Aberdeen line (vid Natal and Capetown), Federal, Houlder, Shire and Bucknall lines (vid Suez). Ten mails per month are sent to and received from England.
