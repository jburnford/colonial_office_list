The dairying industry has made rapid strides in the last few years; in 1905 there were produced 57,606,821 lbs. of butter (of which 38,372,483 lbs. were exported) and 4,297,350 lbs. of cheese. On the 31st Dec., 1905, there were in the State 385,513 horses, 1,088,590 cattle, other than dairy cows, 11,456,115 sheep, and 273,682 pigs. The wool exported in 1905 amounted to 125,181,191 lbs., valued at 5,420,259l. The amount of gold raised in 1905 was 810,050 ozs., making a total since 1851 of 68,367,403 ozs., valued at 273,236,500l. Enormous deposits of brown coal exist, and seams of true coal of good quality which have been discovered are being worked with great success, and it is anticipated that in a short time the State will be nearly independent of outside supplies; the quantity of coal raised in 1905 was 551,885 tons, valued at 379,060l. The chief exports other than wool and gold (which comprise nearly half of the total), are grain and flour, butter, hides and skins, meats, live stock, leather, and tallow, besides a depôt trade in wool, tea, textiles, timber, tobacco, and sugar. The chief imports are wool, gold, textiles and apparel, sugar, live stock, woollens, coal, tea, and iron. Melbourne does 87 per cent. of the trade, the other principal ports being Geelong, Portland, Warrnambool, also Echuca, Wodonga, Wahgunyah, Yarrawonga, Gooramadda, Koondrook, Tocumwal, Cobram, Mildura, and Swan Hill on the Murray river.

The vessels on the Victorian register at the end of 1905 numbered 366, with a total tonnage of 111,029.

Victoria, as a manufacturing country, occupies a pre-eminent position in the Australasian group. The collectors of statistics are instructed to obtain returns only from establishments employing four hands or upwards, except when machinery worked by steam or other power is employed, and except when the existence of industries of an unusual or interesting nature seems to call for special comment. No attempt is made to enumerate mere shops, although some manufacturing industry may be carried on thereat; were this done, the manufactories of the State might be multiplied to an almost indefinite extent. According to the latest returns there are in the State 64 flour mills, which during the year operated upon 10,282,491 bushels of wheat, and 75,595 bushels of other grain; 44 breweries, in which 15,176,439 gallons of beer were brewed; 121 brickyards, some being also potteries, which made 91,495,284 bricks, pipes and tiles valued at 56,086l., and pottery valued at 27,205l.; 88 tanneries, fellmongeries, and wool-washing establishments, which tanned 1,077,346 hides and skins; 11 woollen mills, which used 4,493,041 lbs. of wool and produced 4,093,937 yards of tweed, cloth, and flannel, 145,106 pairs of blankets, and 8,516 shawls; 20 soap and candle works, which made 157,446 cwts. of soap, and 42,049 cwts. of candles; 10 tobacco manufactories, which manufactured 3,981,357 lbs. of tobacco, 167,502 lbs. of cigars, and 237,901 lbs. of cigarettes; 7 distilleries, which distilled 85,690 gallons of spirits; 215 butter and cheese factories, which made 57,606,821 lbs. of butter, 4,297,350 lbs. of cheese, 232,310 gallons of concentrated milk, and sold 16,513 gallons of cream; 26 bacon and ham-curing establishments, which cured 16,433,665 lbs. of bacon and ham; 281 establishments working in books or stationery; 2 in musical instruments; 33 in carving and figures; 12 in designs, medals, and dies; 4 in philosophical instruments; 4 in surgical instruments; 5 in arms and ammunition; 559 in machines, tools, implements, &c., and metals other than gold and silver; 347 in carriages and harness; 10 in ships and boats; 92 in saw-moulding, joinery, &c.; 173 in furniture; 29 in chemicals; 1,145 in textiles and dress; 16 in animal food; 73 in vegetable food; 145 in drinks and stimulants; 187 in chaff cutting, crushing; 21 in oils and fats; 15 in malt; 124 in saw milling, forest; 67 in coal, gas, electric light, etc.; 4 salt works; 15 railway work-shops; 52 in gold, silver, and precious stones; 7 in glass; 1 in asbestos; and 235 in miscellaneous products. The total number of all establishments is 4,264, of which 1,991 use steam or gas engines, the total horse power of which is 43,492. They employ 80,235 hands, and the approximate total value of lands, buildings, machinery, and plant is 13,959,157l. The value of materials used was 15,658,071l., and of articles produced or work done 25,200,648l.

Irrigation.

The climate of Australia is essentially a dry one, and it is to this that its superior salubrity is, to a great extent, attributed. The drawback, however, is that the rainfall is in parts so uncertain that great losses of stock, as well as of crops, occur in some years. Droughts in Victoria are neither so general nor so continuous as in several of the other States. Still, in certain districts, serious inconvenience and loss have been experienced at times on account of deficient rainfall. To obviate this the Government have passed several measures with the view of promoting national irrigation upon a large scale. The more important works, connected with the principal rivers to provide for the supply for local schemes, are undertaken by and are under the direct control of the State. These are known as “National Works,” the total expenditure on which to 30th June, 1905, amounted to 1,134,493l. Local schemes have been carried out by “Irrigation and Water Supply Trusts,” constituted by the Governor in Council, the members of which were elected by the ratepayers. By the Water Act of 1905, all existing irrigation trusts are abolished, their works and duties having been transferred to a State Rivers and Water Supply Commission. Loans are advanced by the Government, and the Commissioners are empowered to levy rates on all lands capable of irrigation within the area under their jurisdiction, in order to provide the annual interest on the loan and the necessary payment to the sinking fund; also to defray the current expenses attendant upon the operations of the trust. On 30th June, 1905, there were 21 of these trusts working, the land under their jurisdiction capable of being irrigated amounting to 2,367,180 acres, of which 165,656 acres were irrigated. The total cost of construction of the works was 1,122,087l.

Chief Towns.

Melbourne, the capital, at the end of 1905, had a population of 515,350. The first settlers established themselves on the site of the present city so recently as in 1835.

The other chief towns are Ballarat (49,648), Bendigo (43,660), Geelong (26,672), Warrnambool (6,600), Castlemaine (8,063), Maryborough (6,000), and Stawell (5,200).
