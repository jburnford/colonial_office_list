On December 31st, 1905, the Cape Government railways consisted of 2,987 miles of line, divided into three systems, known as Western, Midland and Eastern.

(1.) The Western System, starting from Cape Town, extends to Vryburg, a distance of 774 miles.

The following branch lines form part of the Western System:—Wynberg and Simon’s Town (20 miles), serving the populous suburbs of Cape Town and watering places in its vicinity; Malmesbury, Eende Kuil line (110 miles), which leaves the Main Line at Kraaifontein Junction. From Kalabas Kraal on this line there is a 2 feet gauge line to Hopefield vid Darling, a distance of about 47 miles. These two branches serve a very important grain-producing centre. On the main line there are alternative routes between Bellville and Mulder’s Vlei, one of which (the branch, 26 miles) serves the Stellenbosch district. At Eerste River on the Stellenbosch line there is a branch to Sir Lowry’s Pass and Caledon (66 miles), which serves a rich agricultural district.

Another branch leaves the Main Line at Paarl and runs through a fertile district to French Hoek (via Simondium), a distance of 17 miles. At Hutchinson there is a branch, 86 miles long, to Carnarvon. From De Aar a branch line runs to Prieska, 112 miles. From Fourteen Streams there is a line, 4 miles long, to the Transvaal Border, where it connects with the C.S.A. Railway’s new line from the border to Klerksdorp.

The new Cape Central Railway (205 miles), a private line constructed under a Government subsidy, runs from Worcester through Swellendam and Riversdale, to Fore Bay (about 4 miles from Mossel Bay), where it joins the Government line which is being constructed from Mossel Bay to George. The line from Worcester through to Mossel Bay was opened for public traffic on 22nd January, 1906, and is worked by the New Cape Central Railway Company. The line serves several productive agricultural districts.

The Cape Flats and Suburban Railway Syndicate own a short line of railway from Maitland, where it joins the main line of the Cape Government railways, to Ottery Road on the Cape Flats, a distance of 7 miles. By Act 34, 1906, Parliament authorised the acquisition of the existing line by the Government, and the extension thereof from Ottery Road to Diep River (about 2 miles) to join the Simon’s Town line.

A short line, four miles in length, was built by the Milnerton Estates Company, Limited, from Craig’s Crossing, which is situated between Woodstock and Salt River stations, to the Milnerton estates. This branch is worked by the Government on behalf of the Milnerton Estates Company.

The Cape Government railways end at Vryburg, where the lines of the Rhodesia Railways, Limited, commence. The length of line between Vryburg and Bulawayo (Bulawayo exclusive), 588 miles, is worked by the Cape Government Railway Department for the owning company at actual cost, and from Bulawayo northwards the lines are worked by the Beira and Mashonaland Railways.

Railway extension in Rhodesia has been progressing rapidly within the last few years. From Bulawayo there is now through communication to Gwelo, Salisbury, Beira, and the Victoria Falls (280 miles from Bulawayo and 1,642 miles from Cape Town).

By Act 34 of 1906 Parliament authorised the construction of the following lines to further open up the Western Districts of the Colony, viz.:—

Eende Kuil, to a point in the vicinity of Graaf Water or Vogelfontein, in the direction of Van Ryn’s Dorp, a 3 ft. 6 in. gauge line (53½ miles), at a cost of £231,773.

Hopefield, to a point at or near Hoetjes Bay, vid the vicinity of Lange Riet Vlei and the vicinity of Vredenburg (40 miles), at a cost of £87,640. This will be a 2 ft. gauge line.

Belmont to Douglas, a 2 ft. gauge line (55 miles), at a cost of £100,870.

The railway known as the Sea Point Railway (3¼ miles long) has been acquired by the Government, and is being worked under an agreement whereby the Green and Sea Point Municipality guarantee and secure the repayment to the Government, for a period of ten years, of any loss in working, interest on capital, etc.

The Government has constructed a line from Van der Stel, near Somerset West, to Somerset Strand, 2½ miles. It is being worked under guarantee of the Somerset Strand Municipal Council, the arrangement being similar to that under which the Sea Point line is worked.

By Act 34, 1906, Parliament authorised the construction of a line from Ceres Road to Ceres, 10½ miles, at a cost of £60,873, provided the local authority of Ceres enter into an agreement with the Government guaranteeing and securing to the Government the payment for a period of ten years, commencing at the expiration of one year from the date of the opening of the line for traffic, an annual amount not exceeding £1,000, to cover the actual loss incurred by the Government in the working and maintaining of the said line, together with interest at 4% per annum on the capital cost of the line.

By the same Act Parliament has authorised the construction of a line from Bellville to Durbanville, 7½ miles, at a cost of £26,000, the Government being guaranteed against any loss on working, for ten years after the opening of the line for traffic, by the Durbanville Municipality.

The Mafeking Municipality owns a line of railway, 8 miles long, from Mafeking to Buurman’s Drift, on the Transvaal Border, which has been leased by the Government. Provision was made in Act 44 of 1905 for the sum of £84,150 for the construction and equipment of a line of railway to Ottoshoop in the Transvaal, a distance of 22 miles, in order to cater for the trade of the Western Transvaal, the construction of the line to be contingent upon the Cape Government entering into an agreement with the High Commissioner protecting the Colony for a period of not less than ten years against the introduction of lower rates over any lines which might be built by any other administration thereafter to Ottoshoop. Negotiations were entered into with the Transvaal Government with the view of concluding a Convention to this effect, and providing for the construction, equipment, working and maintenance of the portion of the line which would lie in the Transvaal. Parliament, by Act 34, 1906, authorised the utilization of £9,184 of the £84,150 already voted for the acquisition of the line to Buurman’s Drift.
