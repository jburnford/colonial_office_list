British Postal Orders are now payable in Hong Kong and at the British agencies. The postal agencies in China at Canton, Swatow, Amoy, Foochow, Ningpo, Hankow, Hoihow, Chefoo, Tientsin, and at Liu-Kung-Tau, Wei-hai-Wei, are under the superintendence of the Postmaster-General at Hong Kong. There is a branch office at Shanghai.

Education.

The number of Government schools and schools which receive grants from Government is 86, of which 25 are Upper Grade Schools with a staff competent to give instruction in all the subjects of Standard VII, and 61 are Lower Grade Schools under purely native management. This number includes two schools which are reserved for children of European British parentage. Broadly speaking the Upper Grade Schools are taught in English; and the Lower Grade Schools are taught in the Vernacular.

The total number of pupils in average attendance at Government and Grant Schools was 5,353 in 1905. Of these, 1,797 were in Government, and 3,556 in Grant Schools; 3,207 pupils received instruction in English, and 2,146 in the Vernacular. The proportion of boys to girls was 3,401 to 1,952.

Evening Continuation Classes have lately been instituted to supply instruction in commercial and technical subjects.

Revenue.

About one-third of the revenue is derived from the Opium Monopoly and one-fifth from municipal rates for police, lighting, water and other purposes; 13 per cent. on the annual value in Victoria and the Hill Districts; 12½ per cent. at Yau-ma-ti, Kowloon Point, and Hung-Hom; and rates varying from 7 per cent. to 9 per cent. in the outlying villages, payable by the owners. The annual rates payable on the valuation for 1906-7 are $1,395,934·60, an increase of $64,038·76 over those of previous valuation. The remainder is derived from licences, land revenue, stamps, and fees. Land Sales are expected to realize $200,000 in 1907, Stamp Revenue to reach $600,000, and Postal Revenue $418,200. The total Revenue is estimated at $6,448,000 for 1907.

Defence.

There is an Imperial garrison of about 4,000 men, towards the cost of which the Colony contributes 20 per cent. of the public revenue annually. There is a Volunteer Corps, consisting of two companies of Garrison Artillery and one company of Engineers and a Mounted Troop, with a membership of about 280. The Troop, which numbers about 30, has recently been formed for purposes of scouting and orderly duty in time of war. A Volunteer Reserve Association has also been formed, members of which are required to make themselves proficient in rifle shooting, and undertake to enrol under the Volunteer Ordinance in the event of hostilities. The association has a membership of about 220. The port is the headquarters of the China Squadron which combines with the East Indies and Australian Squadrons to form the Eastern Fleet. There is usually one war vessel present besides the Station Receiving Ship. Land batteries have been erected with a view to protecting the town and anchorage. There is an armed police, 1,018 strong in 1905, of whom 133 were Europeans, 382 Indians, and 503 Chinese.

Constitution.

The Government is administered under Letters Patent of 19th January, 1888, by a Governor, aided by an Executive Council, composed of six official and two unofficial members, besides the Governor. The Legislative Council is presided over by the Governor, and is composed of seven official and six unofficial members, three of whom are nominated by the Crown on the recommendation of the Governor (two being usually Chinese), one is nominated by the Justices of the Peace from their body, and one by the Chamber of Commerce. The unofficial members vacate their seats after six years from date of appointment. The “Colonial Courts of Admiralty Act, 1890,” regulates the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court in Admiralty cases. The English Common Law forms the basis of the legal system, modified by Colonial Ordinances, of which a revised edition to 1901 has been issued. The law as to civil procedure was codified by Ordinance No. 3 of 1901.
