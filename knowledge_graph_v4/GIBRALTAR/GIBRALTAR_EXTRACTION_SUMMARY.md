## Gibraltar's Evolution: Key Themes Across 100 Years

### Political/Administrative Evolution
1. **1867-1920**: No Executive or Legislative Council
   - Governor exercises all legislative and executive powers
   - Also serves as General commanding the garrison
   - Only quasi-democratic body: Sanitary Commissioners (partly elected)

2. **1922**: Executive Council established (mentioned in 1920 source as coming)

3. **1948-1950**: Legislative Council announced/established
   - Major democratic milestone
   - Elected members introduced

4. **1950s-1960s**: Developed representative government
   - Legislative Council with elected members (7 elected, 2 nominated)
   - Executive Council with unofficial members
   - Speaker appointed (1958)

### Military Significance (Constant Theme)
- **Throughout all years**: Strategic fortress controlling Straits of Gibraltar
- Royal Navy base and coaling station
- Permanent military garrison (~5,000-6,000 men across most years)
- Naval establishment (~220-400 personnel)
- Described as "one large citadel" - entire territory fortified

### Relationship with Spain
- **Geographic**: Peninsula extending from southwest Spain
- **Border town**: La Linea (Spanish) adjacent to North Front
- **Currency evolution**: 
  - 1867-1897: British sterling (£)
  - 1898-1950s: Spanish pesetas legal tender
  - 1898 onwards: British currency restored, pesetas still circulated
  - 1950s-1960s: Gibraltar Government notes + UK coinage

### Economic Development
1. **Free Port Status**: Maintained throughout (1704-1966)
2. **Primary Industries**:
   - Coaling station (major employer: ~1,200 workers)
   - Tobacco manufacturing (42 factories in 1900, ~600 workers)
   - Ship repairs and services
   - Tourism (emerged strongly post-WWII)

3. **Shipping Statistics** (showing growth):
   - 1888: 12M tons total
   - 1918: 33M tons (WWI peak)
   - 1958: 9.7M tons (vessels: 8,625)

### Population Growth
- 1867: 15,462 (civilian, excl. military 6,638)
- 1881: 18,381 (census)
- 1911: 19,120 (census)
- 1951: 22,848 (census)
- 1958: 25,637 (estimate)

**Note**: WWII evacuation affected 1940s numbers

### Social Services Evolution
1. **Education**:
   - 1867-1916: Not compulsory, church-run schools
   - 1917: Compulsory education introduced
   - 1958: 8 secondary + 15 primary schools, 4,083 pupils

2. **Healthcare**:
   - Early years: Basic medical services
   - 1950s-1960s: Multiple hospitals (Colonial, King George V, Infectious Diseases, St. Joseph's)
   - Total beds 1958: 297

3. **Infrastructure**:
   - 1920: Naval harbor completion (North, South, Detached Moles)
   - 1950s: Airport facilities (RAF North Front)
   - Broadcasting: Radio Gibraltar established 1958

## Unique Characteristics of Gibraltar

### Strategic Importance
- **Only British territory** in Continental Europe (except Malta, which became independent)
- **Controls Straits**: Maritime chokepoint between Atlantic and Mediterranean
- **"The Rock"**: Naturally fortified mountain (1,396-1,439 feet)

### Administrative Uniqueness
- **Military Governance**: Governor always a senior military officer commanding garrison
- **Late Democracy**: No legislative council until 1950 (latest among major colonies)
- **War Office Control**: Except Convict Establishment, under War Office not Colonial Office (1867)

### Economic Model
- **Free Port**: Since British occupation (1704)
- **Service Economy**: Dependent on servicing ships (coaling, repairs, supplies)
- **No Agriculture**: Virtually all food imported
- **Small Manufacturing**: Tobacco processing primary industry

### Cultural/Geographic Context
- **Spanish Border**: Daily cross-border labor from Spain
- **Multilingual**: English official, Spanish widely spoken
- **Consular Hub**: Extensive consular representation (40+ consulates by 1900)
