NEW ZEALAND.

Free passages are granted by the Government of New Zealand.

The classes eligible are married and single agricultural labourers, navvies, shepherds, and mechanics, &c., not exceeding 40 years of age, or if married 45 years of age; also single women as cooks, housemaids, nurses, general servants, dairy-maids, &c., who are not under 15 nor over 35 years of age.

Free passages are not granted to more than 3 children between 1 and 12 years of age in each family.

The following payments for bedding and mess utensils on board ship are required before embarkation:—

Single men and women . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Twenty shillings each.

Married couples . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Twenty shillings each adult.

Children under 12 . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . Ten shillings each.

Under the provisions of the Immigrants’ Land Act, 1873, every person approved by the Agent-General in England, and who pays his own passage to the colony, may claim a free grant of land to the value of 20l. for himself and for any adult member of his family whose passage is also paid. Two children are reckoned as an adult. The Crown Grant of the land is to be conditional on occupation and use, but the immigrant is to be allowed to remain five years in the colony before selecting his land.

For further particulars application may be made to the Agent General for New Zealand, No. 7 Westminster Chambers, Victoria Street, Westminster, London, S.W.
