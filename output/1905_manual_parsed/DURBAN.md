The rainy season is from October to March inclusive, during which thunderstorms are frequent and severe. There is no malaria in the colony. The death rates at Pietermaritzburg and Durban for 1903 were 17·51 and 14·79 per thousand respectively.



About 84 per cent. of the inhabitants are Zulu natives, and about 7·6 per cent. are Indian coolies. The Europeans number 82,542, or 7·9 per cent., of whom about 12,000 are Dutch, the rest being chiefly British, with the exception of about 1,500 colonists of German descent in the New Hanover and Lower Umzimkulu divisions.



Means of Communication.



There is a weekly mail service with England (22 days), with the Cape and Inland Colonies daily, with Mauritius and East Coast ports (Inhambane, Chiloane, Beira, Mozambique, Ibo, etc.) once or twice a month, with Delagoa Bay six times a month or more frequently, with Australia fortnightly, and at intervals of ten or twelve days with India. Steamers pass between Natal and Table Bay every week, calling at East London, Port Elizabeth, and Mossel Bay. There are also two direct lines of steamers between London and Durban, the average passage of which is about 26 days. Two mail and passenger trains leave daily for Cape Town via Johannesburg, the distance being accomplished in 72 hours.



There is direct land telegraph communication with the Transvaal, the Orange River Colony, the Cape, Rhodesia, Delagoa Bay, and other parts of South Africa; and by cable with Zanzibar, Aden, Europe, and the world generally.

Natal Government Railways.



Statistical Summary as at 31st December, 1903.



There are 762 miles of railway open, all constructed and worked by the Government, with the exception of the North Coast extension beyond Verulam to Tugela (50 miles), which was constructed by the Natal-Zululand Railway Company, and an extension of 98 miles from Tugela to Somkele (Hlabisa Coalfields), constructed by Mr. Thomas Reynolds, both of which are worked by the Government, on behalf of the constructors, in accordance with working agreements.



The main line extends from the Port of Durban to Pietermaritzburg (the capital of the Colony, 70 miles inland), and thence to Charlestown, on the border of the Transvaal (306 miles distant from the port), where it connects with the line to Johannesburg and Pretoria.



A branch line runs from Thornville Junction (50½ miles from the port) to Richmond (17 miles), and from Pietermaritzburg a branch connects Greytown with the City (64½ miles).



A branch to Harrismith in the Orange River Colony, from the main line at the 1903 milepost, was opened on the 12th July, 1892, with a mileage of 59½ miles, 23½ of which run through the Orange River Colony.



A branch line runs from Glencoe Junction (231½ miles distant from the port), tapping the Dundee Coalfields, and thence to Vryheid (59½ miles), the Capital of the territory of the late Zululand Republic, recently added to the Colony of Natal.



An extension of the line from Harrismith to Bethlehem (about 60 miles) is in course of construction, and will shortly be opened for traffic, and worked by the Central South African Railways. The section from Van Reenen to Harrismith was purchased by the Transvaal as from the 1st December, 1903, but will in the meantime, under arrangement, continue to be worked by Natal.



A branch extends northwards from Durban to Somkele (167½ miles), in the Province of Zululand.



Another branch runs from South Coast Junction to North Shepstone (77 miles), and places in touch with the town of Durban a portion of the Colony which has large natural resources. This district contains valuable lime and marble deposits.



A line runs from the South Coast Branch at Alexandra Junction to Umtinto, 6½ miles distant, and serves an important sugar-growing area.



A further branch connecting the main line with the Bluff side (Wests) goes out from Clairmont on the South Coast Line, and is 6½ miles in extent.

The work of railway construction was begun in January, 1876, and the sections then authorised were completed in December, 1880. Two subsequent extensions of 119 and 114 miles respectively were sanctioned, and the last, which completes the main line to Charlestown, on the border of the Transvaal, was opened for passenger traffic on 7th April, 1891, and for goods traffic on 1st August, 1891. Connection with Johannesburg and Pretoria was opened on the 1st December, 1896.



Work upon the new connection with Cape Colony, which will effect its junction at Riverside on the Griqualand East boundary, a distance of 100 miles from Maritzburg (the Natal Trunk Line junction), has been commenced, and the first section of about 37 miles will shortly be opened for general traffic.



The total cost of construction of the railways up to the end of 1903 was 10,543,179L. The receipts for 1903 were 2,561,552L., and the expenditure 1,791,108L. The net receipts were equal to 7L. 6s. 180L. per cent. upon the capital expended on open lines.



In addition to the railways owned and worked by the Natal Government there are two private lines in Natal. The Tea Estates of Messrs. J. L. Hulett and Sons, Ltd., are served by a 2 feet narrow gauge railway which branches off from Stanger Station, and is 8 miles in length. The ruling gradient of the railway is 1 in 30.



The Lower Illovo Sugar Estate, situated on the south coast about 22 miles from Durban, possesses a 2 feet narrow gauge line 8 miles in length. The grades vary from 1 to 4:31 in 100.



Defence.



There was, in 1903, an Imperial garrison quartered at Pietermaritzburg, Howick, and Mooi River, consisting of one regiment of cavalry, six batteries of artillery and two infantry battalions, besides detachments of the usual departmental corps. The total strength of the Imperial Forces in Natal, 1903, was 1,150 infantry, 6 batteries artillery—710, 620 cavalry, and details about 200. Total 2,680 men.



The volunteer force of the Colony has been converted into a militia, is partially paid, and is divided into a naval corps of 199 men, about 2,000 mounted riflemen, 3 field batteries of 300, a composite regiment of about 300 men, including a battery of 6 15-pounder B.L. guns, and an infantry battalion of 531 men; total, 3,197 of all ranks (1903). The whole force is fully equipped and armed with modern weapons.



The Colony also maintains an armed and mounted police force, numbering 954 Europeans, fully equipped and available for field service. The volunteers and police were on active service for over 12 months during the Boer War (1899-1902). Since the beginning of 1903 volunteering received a great impetus, owing to the passing of the Militia Act; and in September of that year the total number of all ranks was about 2,700.



Military training is compulsory on all boys over 10 years attending the Government schools. They are organised in cadet corps, and numbered 2,712 at end of 1903. There is also a senior cadet corps, formed during 1904, composed of youths who have left school of from 16 to 20 years of age, and numbering some 320. In addition to this, members of rifle associations are liable to be called out for defence purposes.



The Port of Durban is defended by batteries armed with modern heavy guns.



Industry.



The chief exports are wool, sugar, tea, hides, skins, horns, coal, maize, hair, wattle bark, fruit and tobacco.



There was a large transit trade with the Orange River Colony and the Transvaal previous to the war, which, it is thought, will be enormously increased now that the war is over. The harbour of Durban, which has a bar at its entrance, has been much improved by the works of the Harbour Department, and by dredging. The average depth at low water, for 1903, was 21ft. 3½in. The largest ships are now able to come in and go out at all times with perfect safety. It is a port of registry, having in 1903 33 vessels, with a total tonnage of 2,241 tons (net), in addition to a number of tugs and dredgers owned by Government. The main industry of the Colony is, however, agriculture, large numbers of cattle and sheep being reared, and large crops of maize and sugar raised. The land under cultivation amounted in 1903 to 768,268 acres.

The amount consumed by the railway department in 1903 was 206,327 tons. There is also a considerable and growing trade in bunkered and exported coal.



There were 25 out-putting collieries in 1903.



The present output is at the rate of about 800,000 tons per annum. The value of coal at the pit's mouth is about 12s. a ton, and the freight charged for railway transport is ½d. a ton per mile.



The area under sugar in 1903 was about 33,282 acres, the produce for the year 1903 being about 34,000 tons (a great portion of the sugar-cane is not crushed for sugar, but is grown for the purpose of feeding stock); that under coffee about 181 acres, yielding about 52,490 lbs. Tea, tobacco, arrowroot, wattle trees, and cayenne pepper are also grown. The area under tea in 1903 was 3,895 acres, yielding 1,761,091 lbs., and the area cultivated by natives was 486,489 acres.

Horses, 29,320; mules, 1,598; cattle (horned), 257,632; sheep, 509,978; Angora goats, 115,096; pigs, 11,630; donkeys, 1,652; ostriches, 862.



The stock owned by natives was as follows:



Horses, 24,743; cattle (horned), 365,616; sheep, 109,296; goats, 610,512; pigs, 36,992. Stock owned by Indians: Cattle, 3,479; goats, 13,250; horses, 561; pigs, 1,157; sheep, 28.



The very large decrease in the number of horned cattle is due to the ravages of rinderpest during the latter half of 1897, and, since then, to losses caused by the war, 1899-1902. The disease was stamped out, but has since reappeared in a mild form in several districts.



Currency and Banking.



The currency is exclusively British sterling. The Natal Bank, the Standard Bank of South Africa, the Bank of Africa, the African Banking Corporation, and the National Bank of South Africa have together thirty-four establishments, with 4,406,721L. deposits in 1903. A Government savings bank was established in 1868, and had in 1903 (December 31st) 617,246L. deposits.

Education.



In 1903 there were 440 schools under Government inspection—256 European, 161 native, and 23 Indian—with an aggregate attendance of 22,929. There are 41 Government schools, and 399 Government-aided Schools. The aggregate number of pupils in regular attendance at the Government and Government-aided Schools was 17,657 (1903). The management is vested in the Minister of Education, with the Superintendent of Education as permanent head of the Department, and the local control occasionally in committees. Education is not compulsory. There are Government secondary schools at Pietermaritzburg and Durban, as well as private establishments. The expenditure on education, for the financial year 1902-03, amounted to £82,881. 5s. 11d., and the revenue to £11,913l. 17s. 2d.
