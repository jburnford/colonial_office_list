Prior to this, the Civil Administration was partly organised on a provisional basis, resident magistrates and district Commissioners having been appointed in many districts. Numerous proclamations were issued to provide for administrative requirements, and amongst those issued in 1902 may be mentioned those making provision for the acquisition of lands for public purposes (Proclamation 7), placing subsidised schools under the control of the Director of Education (9), amending the law relating to the registration of deeds (10), levying a tax of 10 per cent. on the annual net produce of gold-bearing produce (34), regulating the registration of mining rights (35, amended by Ordinance 6 of 1902), and several relating to the administration of justice. On the 21st of June, 1902, Letters Patent were published constituting the Government of the Transvaal, and providing for an Executive and a Legislative Council, the members of which are to be appointed by the Crown; the subsequent legislation was accordingly by Ordinance.



On 23rd March, 1903, a Customs Agreement was signed, under which the Transvaal and Southern Rhodesia entered the Customs Union, the result being that a common tariff was established for all the South African Colonies. All transit dues were abolished, and considerable reductions were made in through railway rates. The list of South African products which are carried over the railways at preferential rates—this constituting a second line of Protection—was reduced from 110 to 58 articles. A rebate is granted on articles manufactured in the United Kingdom of 25% of the duty in certain cases and of the whole duty in those cases where it is 2½ ad valorem (ironmongery, etc.); a similar rebate may be made to any British colony giving equivalent reciprocal privileges to the Union.



**Railways.**



The lines of the Netherlands Railway Company (Nederlandsche Zuid Afrikaansche Spoorweg Maatschappij), a company based at Amsterdam in 1887, were at the time of the outbreak of war 717 miles long, consisting of lines connecting the Rand and Pretoria with the Cape and Orange Free State systems, with Natal and with Delagoa Bay. The line from the Cape reached Johannesburg in 1892, and is 52 miles long from the Vaal to Johannesburg. The connections with Natal (Charlestown to Johannesburg, 178 miles) and Delagoa Bay (from Portuguese border to Johannesburg, 341 miles) were opened in 1895. There are also branches from Springs through Johannesburg to Klerksdorp (102 miles), and from Kaupmuiden to Barberton (38 miles). The Pretoria-Pietersburg line, which originally belonged to a London company, is 178 miles long and was opened for traffic in October, 1898. In 1904 the length of open lines in the Transvaal was 915 miles, and 179 miles were under construction.



All these lines now come under the Central South African Railways Administration.*

Krugersdorp to Rustenburg was proceeded with. The Inter-Colonial Council, however, in November ratified certain agreements by which the Transvaal and Orange River Colony will benefit. These were for the construction of (1) a railway from Bethlehem to Kroonstad in the latter colony, forming a link between the Natal system via Ladysmith and Harrismith and the trunk line from Cape Colony to Johannesburg. This line is to be constructed by the Government of Natal. (2) Bethlehem to Modderpoort. (3) Ladybrand to Modderpoort, to be built by the Orange River Colony and the municipality of Ladybrand. (4) Aliwal North, on the Orange River, to Wepener, on the Basutoland frontier, to be built by the Cape Government. The length of these lines is 339 miles.



An agreement has been made with the De Beers Company, under which the Company provide the funds for the construction by the Central South African Railways of a line between Fourteen Streams and Klerksdorp. The estimated cost is £788,464. The Company will control the line, but after five years the railways have the option of expropriating it. This line will bring Johannesburg 312 miles from Kimberley, the present line via De Aar being 661 miles, and also reduces the distance to Port Elizabeth by 153 miles.



The first-class fare from Cape Town to Pretoria is 9l. 14s. 4d., 100lbs. luggage free; second-class, 7l. 4s. 11d., 75lbs. free.



**Agriculture.**



A Lands Department was established in 1902, and deals with the acquisition of land for the purposes of settlement, the importation of cattle for re-stocking, and the various subjects important to scientific farming. A sum of £1,200,000 has been appropriated from loan funds to land settlement, of which about £500,000 has been spent on the purchase of land, and £250,000 on the purchase of stock and equipment and in loans to settlers. The year 1903-4 was more favourable to farmers than the preceding year, abundant rain falling in the summer months.



**Local Government.**



A Municipal Corporations Ordinance was enacted in 1903, which entrusted the local authorities with extensive powers. Interference by the Government is practically limited to the powers of audit. The basis of qualification is the ownership of real property to the value of 100l., or the tenancy of premises of the value of 300l., or annual value of 24l., but the franchise is not extended to aliens or to coloured British subjects. Great progress in the improvement of the towns has already been made.



**Education.**



At the end of 1903 there were 373 primary schools, with 24,972 scholars. There are 14 provincial fee-paying schools, and high schools at Johannesburg and Pretoria.

During Mr. Chamberlain's stay in Johannesburg, an arrangement was made between him and a large representative body of the colonists that a loan of £35,000,000 should be issued, guaranteed by the Imperial Government, and secured on the general revenue of the Transvaal and Orange River Colonies, bearing interest at 3½% per annum, with a sinking fund of 1½%. It was understood that the proceeds of this loan would be used for the extinction of liabilities, the purchase of public works and railways, and other local requirements. It was further understood that a further loan of £30,000,000, bearing interest at 4%, should be issued, and that the proceeds of this loan should be paid to the Imperial Government as a contribution towards the cost of the war. This issue was to take place in three instalments of £10,000,000 each, at an interval of at least twelve months between each, and the first instalment was to be raised about January, 1904, the success of this issue being guaranteed by certain South African firms. The loan was to be a charge on the general revenue of the Transvaal.



The necessary legislation having been passed in the Imperial Parliament and the local Legislature,

the first of these loans, to the extent of 30,000,000£, was issued (at par) in May, 1903. Throughout this year, however, there was considerable depression in the Colony, and, as this state of things continued, His Majesty's Government have not pressed for the raising of the second or war loan.



The balance of the 30,000,000£ loan (5,000,000£) was raised about the middle of 1904. This loan was put up to tender, and realised an average price of 98£ 10s. 3d. By that time the whole of the first issue had been practically expended, counting in the amounts ear-marked for definite impending liabilities—such as the redemption of the shares of the Netherlands Railway Company. The heavy task of repairing the ravages of war and relieving the necessities of all classes of people in the new Colonies was dealt with from four funds, viz:



1. The 3,000,000£ mentioned in the Terms of Surrender, and paid as a free grant from Imperial funds.

2. 5,000,000£ appropriated for "repatriation and compensation" from the guaranteed loan. Both of these funds were fully spent by the middle of 1904, and a debit of about 600,000£ incurred; the expenditure had, however, almost ceased, and it is anticipated that the debit will eventually be extinguished by the repayment for supplies made in excess of the 3,000,000£.

3. 2,000,000£ allotted to compensation to "British subjects, foreign subjects, and natives." This, like (1), was a free grant from Imperial funds. More than half of it was paid by the 30th April, 1904, to persons who had suffered losses from the war, at the rate of 13s. 4d. in the pound on their assessed losses, and it is anticipated that the final instalment will bring the rate up to 17s. 4d., a very remarkable degree of compensation. 200,000£ has been provisionally set aside for natives.

4. 2,000,000£ paid by the War Office for disbursement to the "protected burghers," being men to whom the military authorities had incurred some special obligation.



To this was added a further sum of 2,500,000£ to discharge the military receipts given during the war. This must be distinguished from the previous funds, as it was required for the payment of debts which formed part of the expenses of the war; the payments, however, in common with those under the previous heads, assisted the people to re-build, re-stock, and make a fresh start in their occupations. The amount actually paid has slightly exceeded the 2,500,000£.



Thus the total amount provided for relief was 14,500,000£, of which 2,500,000£ was in payment for goods received.



When the guaranteed loan was about to be issued, several difficulties arose as to the allocation of the proceeds, as there were various matters in which both of the new Colonies were interested, and it was accordingly decided to treat the railways as the joint property of the two Colonies, and to establish a common fund for that and the like common services. For these purposes the Inter-Colonial Council was constituted. (See under "South Africa.") The revenue expenditure of this body for the year 1903-04 was 2,586,548£, the principal items being 1,203,575£, for the services of the guaranteed loan, and 1,317,755£ for the South African Constabulary, which consists of about 5,000 men. Towards this expenditure the Council had a revenue 1,617,254£ from the railways. There was also a revenue of 99,825£ from interest on advances out of guaranteed loan funds. This total revenue being insufficient by about 850,000£, the two Colonies were required to contribute the latter sum, and the two contributions, which under the Order in Council (unless the Secretary of State decides otherwise) are to be in proportion to their respective customs receipts for the preceding year, were 750,000£ from the Transvaal, and 100,000£ from the Orange River Colony.

The Transvaal stood before the outbreak of war at the head of the gold-producing countries of the world.

Almost the whole production is from the reef, the alluvial gold being inconceivable in amount. In 1897, 65 per cent. was obtained by crushing and 34 per cent. by chemical processes. In the same year the average production of gold per ton was 39·79 shillings, and the average number of persons employed was 9,530 whites and 69,127 natives. These numbers rose to 12,000 and 107,000 just before the war in 1899, but then fell to about 10,000 and 45,000 in December, 1902, with 48 mines crushing, and 3,000 stamps running; in

December, 1903, the whites numbered 12,702 and the natives 73,622. The wages in 1903 were from 9s. 5d. to 11s. a day for white labourers, and 42s. 5d. monthly for natives, who are also provided with housing and food, the latter at a cost of about 12s. a month. In July, 1904, there were 1,382 Chinese employed, and by December this number had risen to about 23,000.



The main reef on the Witwatersrand, continuously traced, measures about 62 miles, and between 1887 and end of 1904 produced about 124,000,000l. of gold. Since the British occupation great activity has been displayed in boring operations, especially in the far eastern and western extensions of the Rand; but in the outside districts prospecting is for promising quartz or other surface indications. Considerable prospecting for diamonds is being carried on in the Pretoria district, and the Premier mine, about 20 miles north-east of Pretoria, is producing at the rate of 49,349 carats per month (67,865l.).



In 1903 the output of coal was 2,533,677 tons, valued at 877,976l.



The Title Department has completely indexed diagrams of all mining rights in the colony.
