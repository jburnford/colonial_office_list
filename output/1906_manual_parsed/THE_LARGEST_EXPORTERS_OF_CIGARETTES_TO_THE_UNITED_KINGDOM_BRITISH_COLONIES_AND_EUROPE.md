A FABRIC FOR TROPICAL WEAR.



THE BRITISH MEDICAL JOURNAL says:—"Upon the whole we are inclined to regard it as a successful attempt to solve a by no means easy problem, attention having been duly paid both to the requirements of medical science and to the practical needs of travellers."



THE ARMY AND NAVY GAZETTE, October 14, 1905:—"A good deal of attention has been directed recently in the Press to the best means of protection from heat in tropical climates, and the idea, which at first sight seems anomalous, has been propounded that red clothing or red-lined fabrics should be used to protect the body from the actinic rays of the sun, as the sensitive plate is protected by photographers. The matter is one of considerable importance to soldiers, many of whom have to pass prolonged periods in hot countries. The idea was often proposed by Anglo-Indian physicians of the last century, and Colonel Maude has told the readers of The Pall Mall Gazette what benefit he has experienced personally in using red-lined clothing, and how difficult he has found it to convince the authorities of facts that were scientifically demonstrable. To the same paper Messrs. Ellis & Johns, who have introduced a special red or orange fabric for use in tropical climates, have written pointing out that the idea was not 'made in Germany,' as the previous correspondents had suggested, but originated in India, where their fabrics were introduced two years ago by the Colonial Office for the use of officers appointed to hot countries. The question of health and comfort is of such importance to the Army that we hope the military authorities will inquire into it. The problems concerned are certainly not easy of solution, but our excellent contemporary, The British Medical Journal, considers that a considerable step has been made to the solution of the difficulty, and no effort should be left untried to secure the health and comfort of His Majesty's servants, compelled, as soldiers are, to live in tropical climates."
