MONTSERRAT.



This Island, discovered by Columbus in 1493, was named by him after a famous mountain in Spain, where is situated the monastery in which Ignatius Loyola conceived the project of founding the Society of Jesus.



It is situated 27 miles from Antigua in 16°45' N. lat., and 61° W. long., and is about 11 miles in length, and 7 in the broadest part. Its total area is 321 square miles. It is of volcanic formation and very rugged and mountainous. The hills are covered with forest, the highest elevation being Soufriere Hill (3,000 feet) in the southern part of the island. Plymouth, the chief town, with a population of 1,461, is on an open roadstead on the south-west coast. It is a port of registry, and had, on 31st December, 1906, 5 sailing vessels, total tonnage 74 tons net.

Montserrat was colonised by the English in 1632; the French took it in 1664, and levied heavy imposts on the inhabitants; it was restored to England in 1668. It capitulated to the French in 1782, but was restored to the English in 1784.



Montserrat possessed a Legislative Council and Assembly as early as 1668. After undergoing various modifications they became merged in a Legislative Assembly, which lasted down to 1867.



By an Act of the Legislative Assembly, dated 24th Dec., 1866, and confirmed by Order in Council 26th Feb., 1867, instead of a Legislative Assembly, a Legislative Council was established, having all the powers, authorities, and rights hitherto possessed by the Assembly. By Act No. 2, of 1902, which rescinded these Acts, His Majesty may, from time to time, appoint such persons, not exceeding four in all, as he may think fit, every Councillor holding office during His Majesty's pleasure.



The cultivation of sugar, once the staple industry of the island, has declined to such an extent that lime-juice and cotton are now the principal products, the exports in 1906 amounting to 109,427 gallons of raw, 8,504 gallons of concentrated juice, and 8 tons of citrate of lime, of a total value of £6,278l. There are about 1,000 acres planted in lime trees, and the cultivation is being yearly extended. The industry is principally in the hands of the Montserrat Company Limited. A trade in fresh limes, which are shipped to London, has also sprung up, and is likely to assume considerable proportions in the near future.



The cultivation of cotton (which was once grown during the American Civil War), was re-introduced in 1901, and, where the lands are suitable, has ousted the sugar industry, the exports in 1906 amounting to 126,733 lbs. of lint, valued at £7,921l., while the sugar shipped amounted to 350 tons only, of the value of £3,379l. The industry is being further expanded, all suitable lands being cultivated.



The output of sugar is now small. Whole estates which were once sugar plantations are now cultivated in cotton and limes.



A fruit preserving industry has been established, and a certain amount of fresh pineapples are exported annually to the English markets. Among the other exports may be mentioned a drug known as "papain," and essential oils, which are exported to a limited extent.



The cultivation of coffee and cocoa which, had declined to such an extent that enough for home consumption was not grown, is being revived, and 7,376 lbs. of cocoa, of the value of £215l., was exported.



There are seven Anglican schools with 2,000 scholars, three Wesleyans with 907, one Roman Catholic with 307, and one undenominational with 335 scholars.



The roads of the island have of late years been much improved, macadamised, and drained by masonry culverts and surface drains; and every year progress is made in these respects, as far as the small revenue of the island will permit.



On 7th August, 1899, the island was devastated by a fearful hurricane.



The island is considered to be the most healthy of the Antilles.



The rainfall in 1906 was 65·81 inches (mean of 14 stations); and the mean temperature is about dry bulb 79·45°, wet bulb 73·79°.
