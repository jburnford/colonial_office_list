Montserrat.

This island, discovered by the celebrated Spanish navigator in 1433, was named by him after a mountain in Catalonia, to which it is said to bear a very striking resemblance.

It is situated in 16° 45' N. lat., and 61° W. long.; it is about 12 miles in length, and 8 in the broadest part; its roads however are zigzag, uphill, and down ravine, so that a journey from the town of Plymouth (lying in the centre of the south-west coast), to north or south, exceeds the extreme length of the island.

Montserrat was colonized by the English in 1632; the French took it in 1664, and levied heavy imposts on the inhabitants: it was restored to England in 1668. It capitulated to the French in 1782. The articles of capitulation were signed by the Lieutenant-Governor of the island, and by M. le Comte Flechin and M. le Comte de Barras. M. Louis Joseph de Goulion was appointed Commandant, who levied an impost of 1800 Johannisés, demanded a furnished residence for himself, one for the Major, and others for the Treasurer, the Commissary of Marine, for the Surgeon-Major, for warehouses of every kind, for a hospital, for a chapel for Divine service for the troops, and a residence near it for the chaplain, a residence for the keeper of the warehouses, and a chamber for his clerk. The House of Assembly provided the Governor with a furnished house, with horses, &c., &c., but refused the other demands, "as being inconsistent with the laws," as being not bound "with the terms of capitulation, and as having
no power to dispossess the inhabitants of their houses."

The island was restored to the English in 1784.

Montserrat possessed a Legislative Council and Assembly as early as 1668.

In 1861 the Constitution was abolished, by the local legislature.

Montserrat is justly considered the most healthy of the Antilles.

The staple commodity is sugar, which until lately was brought to the town in bags on the backs of mules, or oxen. Carts are now pretty generally used, but the mule and the ox with the bag of sugar on its back is still occasionally seen. There is no harbour, but a very good roadstead.

The scenery of the island is exceedingly beautiful.

The Government is administered by a President (subordinate to the Governor-General of the Leeward Islands), aided by an Executive Council appointed by the Crown. The Legislative Assembly consists of 12 members, of which two-thirds are elected by the people, and one-third nominated by the Crown. The qualification for elective members of the Assembly is—The possession in fee simple or fee entail of an estate in the island, of the annual value of £25 per annum, or possessed of an income of the yearly value of £100.

The qualification for voters is the possession in fee simple of one acre of land, with a cottage attached, and being resident thereon, or of twenty acres of land with or without a dwelling-house, or having a freehold property of the annual value of £10 per annum, or tenant paying not less than £20. A registry of electors is not completed. The Constitution Act of 1862 gives the Officer administering the Government the power of presiding over the deliberations of this body, whenever he may consider it necessary to do so.

**Coins.**

The coins in circulation are British gold and silver doubloons, American and Spanish dollars, American gold pieces.
