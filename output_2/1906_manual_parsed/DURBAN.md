The rainy season is from October to March inclusive, during which thunderstorms are frequent and severe. There is no malaria in the colony. The death rates of Europeans or whites, including those classed in the Census returns
as mixed and others, at Pietermaritzburg and Durban for 1904 were 14·77 and 12·61 per thousand respectively, and for the Colony 10·89.

About 82 per cent. of the inhabitants are Zulu natives, and about 9 per cent. are Indian coolies. The Europeans number 97,109, or 8·8 per cent., of whom about 12,500 are Dutch, the rest being chiefly British, with the exception of about 4,500 colonists of German descent, of whom the greater portion are settled in the New Hanover and Lower Umzimkulu divisions.

Means of Communication.

There is a weekly mail service with England (20 to 22 days), with the Cape and Inland Colonies daily, with Mauritius and East Coast ports (Inhambane, Chiloane, Beira, Mozambique, Ibo, etc.) once or twice a month, with Delagoa Bay six times a month or more frequently, with Australia fortnightly, and at intervals of ten or twelve days with India. Steamers pass between Natal and Table Bay every week, calling at East London, Port Elizabeth, and Mossel Bay. There are also two direct lines of steamers between London and Durban, the average passage of which is about 26 days. Two mail and passenger trains leave daily for Cape Town via Johannesburg, the distance being accomplished in 72 hours.

There is direct land telegraph communication with the Transvaal, the Orange River Colony, the Cape, Rhodesia, Delagoa Bay, and other parts of South Africa; and by cable with Zanzibar, Aden, Europe, and the world generally.

Natal Government Railways.

Statistical Summary as at 31st December, 1904.

There are 775¾ miles of railway open, all constructed and worked by the Government, with the exception of the North Coast extension beyond Verulam to Tugela (50 miles), which was constructed by the Natal-Zululand Railway Company, and an extension of 98 miles from Tugela to Somkele (Hlabisa Coalfields), constructed by Mr. Thomas Reynolds, both of which are worked by the Government, on behalf of the constructors, in accordance with working agreements.

The main line extends from the Port of Durban to Pietermaritzburg (the capital of the Colony, 70 miles inland), and thence to Charlestown, on the border of the Transvaal (306 miles distant from the port), where it connects with the line to Johannesburg and Pretoria.

The work of railway construction was begun in January, 1876, and the sections then authorised were completed in December, 1880. Two subsequent extensions of 119 and 114 miles respectively were sanctioned, and the last, which completes the main line to Charlestown, was opened for passenger traffic on 7th April, 1891, and for goods traffic on 1st August, 1891. Connection with Johannesburg and Pretoria was opened on the 1st December, 1895.

A branch line runs from Thornville Junction (59¾ miles from the port) to Richmond (17 miles), and from Pietermaritzburg a branch connects Greytown with the City (64¾ miles).

A branch to Harrismith in the Orange River Colony, from the main line at the 190¾ milepost, was opened on the 12th July, 1892, with a mileage of 59¾ miles, 23¼ of which run through the Orange River Colony. This section of 23¼ miles between Van Reenen and Harrismith, originally constructed and still worked by the Government, is now owned by the Central South African Railway, having been purchased by the Transvaal as from the 1st December, 1903.

An extension of the line from Harrismith to Bethlehem (65 miles) is open for traffic. The section is, however, still in the hands of the Construction Department. When complete, the line will be handed over to the Natal Government, to be worked under the arrangements applicable to the Van Reenen-Harrismith section.

A branch line runs from Glencoe Junction (231¾ miles distant from the port), tapping the Dundee Coalfields, and thence to Vryheid (69¾ miles), the Capital of the territory of the late Zululand Republic, recently added to the Colony of Natal.

A branch extends northwards from Durban to Somkele (167¾ miles), in the Province of Zululand.

Another branch runs from South Coast Junction to North Shepstone (77 miles), and places in touch with the town of Durban a portion of the Colony which has large natural resources. This district contains valuable lime and marble deposits.

A line runs from the South Coast Branch at Alexandra Junction to Umtinto, 6½ miles distant, and serves an important sugar-growing area.

A further branch connecting the main line with the Bluff side (Wests) goes out from Clairmont on the South Coast Line, and is 6½ miles in extent.

Work upon the new connection with Cape Colony, which will effect its junction at Riverside on the Griqualand East boundary, a distance of 100 miles from Maritzburg (the Natal Trunk Line junction), has been commenced, and the first section of about 37½ miles is now open for traffic. A further section of 42½ miles, it is expected, will be completed towards the end of the year.

The following are other extensions in Natal now in course of construction:—North Shepstone (terminus of South Coast line) to Marburg, 10 miles; Ennersdale (154 miles Main line) to Los Kop, 13 miles.

The total cost of construction of the railways up to the end of 1904 was 11,170,487L. The receipts for 1904 were 1,933,934L., and the expenditure 1,531,210L. The net receipts were equal to 34L. 12s. 1½d. per cent. upon the capital expended on open lines.

In addition to the railways owned and worked by the Natal Government there are two private lines in Natal. The Tea Estates of Messrs. J. L. Hulett and Sons, Ltd., are served by a 2 feet narrow gauge railway which branches off from Stanger Station, and is 8 miles in length. The ruling gradient of the railway is 1 in 30.

The Lower Illovo Sugar Estate, situated on the south coast about 22 miles from Durban, possesses a 2 feet narrow gauge line 8 miles in length. The grades vary from 1 to 4·81 in 100.
Defence.

There was, in 1904, an Imperial garrison quartered at Pietermaritzburg, Howick, and Mooi River, consisting of one infantry battalion, besides detachments of the usual departmental corps. The total strength of the Imperial Forces in Natal, 1904, was 824, including details, of all ranks.

The volunteer force of the Colony has been converted into a militia, is partially paid, and is divided into a naval corps of 158 men, about 2,000 mounted riflemen, 4 field batteries of 356, and 2 infantry battalions of 778 men; total, 3,449 of all ranks (1904). The whole force is fully equipped and armed with modern weapons.

The Colony also maintains an armed and mounted police force, numbering 1,025 Europeans, fully equipped and available for field service. The volunteers and police were on active service for over 12 months during the Boer War (1899-1902). Since the beginning of 1903 volunteering received a great impetus, owing to the passing of the Militia Act; and in September of that year the total number of all ranks was about 2,700.

Military training is compulsory on all boys over 10 years attending the Government schools. They are organised in cadet corps, and numbered 2,923 at end of 1904. There is also a senior cadet corps, formed during 1904, composed of youths who have left school from 16 to 20 years of age, and numbering some 442. In addition to this, members of rifle associations are liable to be called out for defence purposes. At the end of 1904 there were 76 Rifle Associations, with a total strength, excluding Natal police and militiamen, of 2,678.

The Port of Durban is defended by batteries armed with modern heavy guns.

Industry.

The chief exports are wool, sugar, tea, hides, skins, horns, coal, maize, hair, wattle bark, fruit, ale and beer, and tobacco.

There was a large transit trade with the Orange River Colony and the Transvaal previous to the war, which, it is hoped, will be enormously increased in future years. The harbour of Durban, which has a bar at its entrance, has been much improved by the works of the Harbour Department, and by dredging. The average depth at low water, for 1904, was 25 ft. 9 in. The largest ships are now able to come in and go out at all times with perfect safety. It is a port of registry, having in 1904 23 vessels, with a total tonnage of 2,160 tons (net), in addition to a number of tugs and dredgers owned by Government. The main industry of the Colony is, however, agriculture, large numbers of cattle and sheep being reared, and large crops of maize and sugar raised. The land under cultivation amounted in 1904 to 729,712 acres.

There were 23 out-putting collieries in 1904.

The present output is at the rate of about 900,000 tons per annum. The value of coal at the pit's mouth is about 12s. a ton, and the freight charged for railway transport is ½d. a ton per mile.

The area under sugar in 1904 was about 16,513 acres, the produce for the year 1904 being about 19,000 tons (a great portion of the sugar-cane is not crushed for sugar, but is grown for the purpose of feeding stock); that under coffee about 157 acres, yielding about 24,859 lbs. Tea, tobacco, arrowroot, wattle trees, and cayenne pepper are also grown. The area under tea in 1904 was 4,064 acres, yielding 2,406,367 lbs., and the total area cultivated by natives was 400,088 acres.

The very large decrease in the number of horned cattle is due to the ravages of rinderpest during the latter half of 1897, and, since then, to losses caused by the war, 1899-1902. The disease was stamped out, but has since reappeared in a mild form in several districts.

Currency and Banking.

The currency is exclusively British sterling. The Natal Bank, the Standard Bank of South Africa, the Bank of Africa, the African Banking Corporation, and the National Bank of South Africa have together thirty-five establishments, with 4,342,385l. deposits in 1904. A Government savings bank was established in 1868, and had in 1904 (December 31st) 560,659l. deposits.

Education.

In the year ended 30th June, 1904, there were 452 schools under Government inspection—257 European, 9 coloured, 158 native, and 28 Indian—with an aggregate attendance of 22,929. There are 41 Government schools, and 411 Government-aided Schools. The aggregate number of pupils in regular attendance at the Government and Government-aided Schools was 23,691 (1903-04).

The management is vested in the Minister of Education, with the Superintendent of Education as permanent head of the Department, and the local control occasionally in committees. Education is not compulsory. There are Government secondary schools at Pietermaritzburg and Durban, as well as private establishments. The expenditure on education, for the financial year 1903-04, amounted to 98,748l. 7s. 9d., and the revenue to 13,796l. 18s. 1d.
