VICTORIA LEAGUE FOR COMMONWEALTH FRIENDSHIP (Founded 1901)

Victoria League House, 38 Chesham Place, Belgrave Square, London, S.W.1.
Telephone: BELgravia 2201-5.

General Secretary—Colonel R. W. Hills, O.B.E., M.C.

The Victoria League is a voluntary organization to promote individual friendship, knowledge and understanding among people of the Commonwealth, irrespective of their race, creed or political opinion. It has branches and correspondents throughout the Commonwealth, with a membership of over 33,000.

The League helps, in any way they may need, all visitors from the Commonwealth overseas. Its activities include the arranging of hospitality in private homes.

A literature department despatches books and newspapers overseas, arranges lectures to schools and other organizations and links school-children by correspondence.

In addition there is a Students' Club which is for the benefit of students of both sexes from all countries of the Commonwealth. The address is: 55 Leinster Square, London, W.2. Telephone: Bayswater 3961.

The Committee, which was established circa 1750 and incorporated by Royal Charter in 1904, is an association of British subjects, wherever resident, who are personally interested in The West Indies, British Guiana and British Honduras. The object of the West India Committee is to promote the general welfare of those territories and by united action to further their interests.

Members receive The Chronicle of the West India Committee, published monthly.

The Committee's Rooms contain a comprehensive West Indian reference library. Reading and writing rooms, etc., are also available to West Indian visitors to London.

WOMEN'S CORONA SOCIETY

The Women's Corona Society is a voluntary and non-political association of women of all races. Originally its members were all directly or indirectly connected with Her Majesty's Overseas Civil Service and the public services in Colonial territories, but it is now open to any woman in active sympathy with the aims of the Society.

These are: to be a link between its members throughout the Commonwealth; to be of service to them and, through them, to the wider community, with particular emphasis on helping to promote friendship between the people of the Commonwealth. In Britain this is done by giving hospitality and help to members and other women from overseas, not only by the Headquarters Office in London but also in Edinburgh, West of England and Oxford where groups have been formed. If those going overseas are too far from any of these centres, Headquarters will put them in touch by post with a member on leave from the territory in which they are interested and will supply them with up-to-date information on living conditions overseas.

Further help over problems in a new country, for those coming to Britain or going overseas, is provided by three-day courses held in May and October, and one-day conferences and discussion groups in the intervening months. These are open to anyone who may be interested; besides their instructional side of lectures and films, they provide a chance for women of many territories to meet informally over tea or coffee. For members there are other social occasions such as the annual dinner and garden party, lunch meetings and evening parties, which serve as a link between those who carry on the work of the Society. Among its many services is the meeting of and looking after children in transit through London; the compiling of information about holiday homes for children and a register of accommodation.

A Newsletter is circulated three times a year reporting the activities of the Society in Britain and the forty Corona Bodies overseas where local representatives welcome newcomers, helping them to get to know the women of the country and take part with them in giving voluntary service wherever there is most need.

The subscription is 10s. a year or £4 for life membership. For further particulars, apply to the Organizing Secretary.
