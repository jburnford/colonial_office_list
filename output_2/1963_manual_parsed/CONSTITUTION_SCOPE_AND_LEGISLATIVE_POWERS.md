CONSTITUTION, SCOPE AND LEGISLATIVE POWERS

The East African Common Services Organization (Headquarters are at Nairobi, Kenya) was established by an agreement between the Governments of Tanganyika, Kenya and Uganda, signed on 9th December 1961. It replaced the East Africa High Commission in administering the inter-territorial services of the three East African territories and is entirely independent of Her Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom in respect of its own scheduled functions.

The principal functions of the East African Common Services Organization are:—

(a) to administer the following services:—

(1) The East African Railways and Harbours Administration.

(2) The East African Posts and Telecommunications Administration.

(3) The East African Directorate of Civil Aviation.

(4) The East African Meteorological Department.

(5) The East African Customs and Excise Department.

(6) The East African Income Tax Department.

(7) The East African Research Services.

(8) The East Africa Office in London.

(9) The East African Industrial Council.

(10) The East African Statistical Department.

(11) The East African Literature Bureau.

(12) The East African Accountant-General's Department.

(13) The East African Auditor-General's Department.

(14) Inter-territorial services arising from the operations of the East African Currency Board.

(15) The Thika Road House.

(16) The East African Hides and Leather Bureau.

(17) Services arising out of the functions of the East African Common Services Authority as East African Air Authority.

(18) Inter-territorial services financed in part or whole by means of grants made to the Organization from the East African Regional Allocation under the Colonial Development and Welfare Acts.

(19) Services for the administration of such grants made by the Government of any country, any international organization or any authority, for the purposes of inter-territorial projects as are agreed between the East African Common Services Authority and the Governments of the Territories.

(20) Services for the purposes of co-ordinating the economic activities of the Governments of the Territories.

(21) Services for the purposes of any body or authority established in pursuance of sub-paragraph (b) of paragraph 1 or sub-paragraph (d) of paragraph 2 of Article I of the Organization's Constitution.

(22) The Secretariat of the Organization.

(23) Services for the purposes of the performance of the functions of the Assembly, the Public Service Commissions or any officer of the Organization under Part III, IV or V of the Organization's Constitution.

(24) Such other services as may, with the consent of the Government of the United Kingdom and the Governments of the Territories, be designated by the Authority by order published in the Gazette of the Organization.

(b) to provide machinery to facilitate the coordination of the activities of the Governments of Tanganyika, Kenya and Uganda on any matter of common interest to them;

(c) to enact measures relating to matters for which the Organization is responsible to which legislative effect may be given in Tanganyika, Kenya and Uganda.

Responsibility for the Organization's policy rests with the East African Common Services Authority—i.e. the principal elected ministers of
Tanganyika, Kenya and Uganda—which has the capacity of a body corporate with perpetual succession. The Authority is supported by four groups, each composed of three ministers (one from each territory) who are responsible for formulating policy in the specified fields of communications, finance, commercial and industrial co-ordination and social and research services.

The Organization's Central Legislative Assembly is comprised of the twelve ministers making up the four groups supporting the Authority, nine members from each territory elected by the territorial legislature and the Secretary-General and Legal Secretary of the Organization. The Central Legislative Assembly is empowered to pass measures relating to the following matters:

1. Accounts of the Organization and the officers and authorities thereof, including audit of accounts; management and control of the funds of the Organization.
2. Appropriations out of the funds of the Organization (other than the Distributable Pool Fund).
3. Civil Aviation.
4. Customs and Excise—administrative and general provisions (but not including tariff rates).
5. Income Tax—administrative and general provisions (but not including rates of tax and allowances).
6. Powers, privileges and immunities of the Central Legislative Assembly and the members thereof.
7. Inter-territorial Research.
8. Makerere College; the University College of Dar es Salaam; the Royal College, Nairobi, and other institutions of a university character.
9. Meteorology.
10. Pensions, gratuities and other like benefits payable out of the funds of the Organization.
11. Staff of the Organization, including Public Service Commissions of the Organization.
12. Posts and telegraphs, telephones and radio communications.
13. Railways, harbours and inland water transport.
14. Borrowing for the purposes of the Organization.
15. Merchant shipping.
16. Legal proceedings by or against the Organization, the Authority, the Ministerial Committees, or any offices or authority of the Organization.
17. Statistics.
18. The matters with respect to which the Organization is empowered to make provision by paragraph 2 of Article I and by Articles 34 and 36 of its Constitution.
19. Any matter, not mentioned above, that is incidental to the execution, performance or enforcement of any function conferred by the Organization's Constitution, or by an Act of the Organization enacted in accordance with the provisions of its Constitution, upon the Organization or any authority, officer or servant of the Organization.

The enactment of measures of the Organization is effected by means of bills passed by the Central Legislative Assembly and assented to on behalf of the Organization by the Governor-General of Tanganyika, the Governor of Kenya and the Governor of Uganda on the recommendation of the East African Common Services Authority. Although Her Majesty's Government has no powers of direct control over the Organization, the Governors of Kenya and Uganda may be instructed to withhold assent to any of the Organization's measures which impinge on the defence and internal security of Kenya and Uganda or which may conflict with the international obligations of Her Majesty's Government.

The establishment of the East African Common Services Organization has involved no change in the constitution or administrative responsibilities of the Governments of the three territories, which remain responsible for the basic services such as administration, police, health, education, agriculture, animal health, forestry, labour, housing and public works.

The East African Railways and Harbours Administration and the East African Posts and Telecommunications Administration are self-contained and self-financing services. The other services (non-self-contained) administered by the Organization are financed from a Distributable Pool under the Organization's control, which is derived from a proportion of the customs and excise revenue collected in the three territories and a proportion of the income tax on the profits of certain types of companies.
