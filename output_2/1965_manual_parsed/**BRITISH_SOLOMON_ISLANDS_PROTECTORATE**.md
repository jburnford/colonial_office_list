**BRITISH SOLOMON ISLANDS PROTECTORATE**

**Area**

The Protectorate is enclosed within an area of approximately 249,000 square nautical miles, of which about 11,500 square miles are land.

**Principal Town**

Honiara, the capital, is on the island of Guadalcanal and has a population of approximately 3,500. Europeans number approximately 400, Chinese approximately 280.

**Geographical Features**

The Protectorate consists of a double row of mountainous islands situated between the parallels of 5° and 12° 30' S., and the meridians of 155° and 170° E. and includes the islands of Guadalcanal, Malaita, San Cristobal, New Georgia, Santa Ysabel, Choiseul, Mono (or Treasury), Shortlands, Vella Lavella, Ranongga, Simbo, Gizo, Rendova, Kolombangara, Russell, Florida, Rennell, and numerous small islands (Lord Howe Group or Ontong Java, the Santa Cruz Islands, Vanikoro, Tikopia, Mitre Island and the Duff and Reef Groups, etc.).

**Climate**

The seasons are generally well defined by trade winds. The south-east season lasts from April until November when the minimum temperatures are recorded. The highest mean and maximum temperatures and the highest rainfall are, as a rule, recorded in the north-west season from November to April.

**History**

The Solomon Islands were discovered in 1568 by Alvaro de Mendana, while on a voyage of discovery from Peru. Tasman discovered the Ontong Java group in 1643, and in 1767 Captain Carteret re-discovered the Santa Cruz group and the north-west coastline of the island of Malaita. From this time many Europeans visited the group and British ships of war paid periodical visits.

Between 1860 and 1893 the number of resident white traders gradually increased, until at the time of the proclamation in 1893 of the British protectorate over the southern Solomon Islands (Guadalcanal, Savo, Malaita, San Cristobal, the New Georgia group and its dependencies) the number of white residents approached 50. In 1898 and 1899 the islands of the Santa Cruz group, including Utupua, Tikopia, Vanikoro, the remote islands of Cherry and Mitre, Sikaiana, and the islands of Rennell and Bellona were added to the Protectorate. In 1900 the northern islands (Santa Ysabel, Choiseul, the islands of the Shortland group and the atoll group of Ontong Java) were transferred under convention from Germany to Great Britain.
In 1942 Japanese troops invaded the islands. Most of the non-native population had been evacuated to Australia but the Resident Commissioner and a number of his officers remained to continue the Administration as far as possible. The Japanese occupied the central Solomons and were driven back by the Allies only after violent air, sea and land battles in 1942 and 1943; they were not completely dislodged from the north-western extremity of the Protectorate until the surrender of Japan. The effect upon the population of contact with the very large numbers of Allied servicemen in occupation of the islands and based there for operations further afield, was great and disruption of native life was inevitable.

An outstanding feature of the war and the enemy invasion of the territory was the great loyalty of the Islanders to their British connexion and to the Allied cause generally.

**Constitution**

On 18th October 1960 a Constitution which provided for the establishment of a Legislative Council and an Executive Council was brought into operation under the British Solomon Islands Constitution Order in Council, 1960.

The Legislative Council is ordinarily presided over by the High Commissioner for the Western Pacific as President.

The 1960 Constitution provided for three *ex officio* members, not more than eight Official Members and not more than ten Unofficial Members. The Official and Unofficial Members of the Legislative Council were appointed by the High Commissioner in his discretion.

Proposals for the election of a majority of the Unofficial Members were put forward by the Council during 1964 and on 4th November 1964, the first stage of a revised Constitution, embodied in the British Solomon Islands Order in Council, 1964, was brought into operation. This Constitution provides for three *ex officio* members; eight official members; eight elected members; and two nominated (unofficial) members. In seven constituencies the member will be elected through an electoral college composed of elected members of the Local Councils in the area; in Honiara election will be direct.

The Executive Council consists of three *ex officio* members, who are the Chief Secretary, the Attorney-General and the Financial Secretary, and one official and four unofficial members appointed by the High Commissioner.

Legislation is ordinarily made by the High Commissioner with the advice and consent of the Legislative Council.

**Land Policy**

A Land and Titles Ordinance was enacted and brought into force early in 1963. It provides a system of guaranteed title to land but continues to restrict the alienation to non-natives of land held under systems of native customary tenure and permits the owners of such land, if they so wish, to obtain registered title to their land. The Ordinance provides for vacant land, after adjudication if vacancy is contested, to be acquired and vested in a Land Trust Board.

**Taxation**

Income tax is levied on chargeable income on a sliding scale, rising from 9d. in the £ on the first £200 to 15s. in the £ on income over £10,000. No super tax is payable. Companies pay 1s. 6d. in the £ on chargeable income, except for shipping companies and insurance companies, which pay 4½d. and 9d. in the £ respectively.

Local authority rates, payable by all residents on a per capita basis, vary from 10s. to £5 per annum, according to area.

Customs duties represent the chief source of revenue from indirect taxation.

The currency is Australian. This is interchangeable with sterling at £Stg. 1 = £A1. 5s. 0d.

**Development Plan**

The Development Plan 1963–66 is designed to develop the natural and human resources of the Protectorate with the object of strengthening its economy to enable the standards of living of all sections of the community to be raised and in addition to raise the general standard of education. Total expenditure of £A3,015,000 is envisaged of which two-thirds is being financed from Colonial Development and Welfare funds. The amounts allocated to the various services are as follows:

Particular emphasis is being placed on the diversification of the economy by the development of the country's forestry resources, the establishment of cocoa as a second cash crop and the execution of an airborne geophysical survey of the mineral resources. The main expenditure on social services is on the malaria eradication pilot project which covers one third of the population and on the Education Plan which is aimed at raising the level of primary education.

**Education**

Education at the primary level is mainly in the hands of the Churches. In 1963 the Legislative Council adopted an Education Plan which aims to produce an adequate flow of suitably educated pupils for secondary education, by giving maximum support to a limited number of junior and senior primary schools, to be known as designated schools. In 1963 subsidies to Church Schools,
largely salary subsidies for qualified teachers, totalled £A16,837. In 1963 there were four Government primary schools and one Government secondary boarding school, taking pupils to Cambridge Overseas G.C.E. level; in 1962 and 1963, nine boys gained Cambridge Overseas School Certificates. There were approximately 500 pupils in these schools. In addition there were 419 registered Church and Local Authority Schools, with about 18,500 pupils. Since 1959 two-year courses for teachers in junior primary schools (Standards I-IV) have been provided at the British Solomons Training College; the annual intake is 24. The Roman Catholic teacher training institute at Villa Maria is recognised by Government for the training of women teachers; in 1963 fourteen women were enrolled.

Libraries and Museums

There is a small museum in Honiara. A library is maintained by an independent club of subscribers.

Health

The principal endemic diseases are malaria, tuberculosis, hookworm and leprosy.

Apart from the Central Hospital in Honiara, which has accommodation for 140 patients, and the Government Leprosarium at Tetere (100 patients), district hospitals are situated at Auki, Gizo and Kira Kira, and rural hospitals at Malu'u and Tetaba, Santa Ysabel. Fourteen Rural Health units (combined health clinics and maternity and child health centres) were completed or under construction; there are also 61 health clinics and 30 maternity and child health centres.

The various Church authorities also contribute largely to health matters in the Protectorate, there being two Mission Hospitals, each in charge of a Medical Officer, situated at Munda and Faubau.

Yaws has virtually disappeared from large parts of the Protectorate, since the W.H.O./U.N.I.C.E.F. assisted campaign in 1958, which reduced the incidence of the disease from 14.7% to 0.29%. An intensive effort was made towards the end of 1962 to deal with residual pockets of infectious yaws in N.E. Malaita. A mass re-survey of the infected areas treated 26 cases found in 15,300 people and at the end of 1963 a further re-survey showed a drop to 0.26% incidence.

A sister tutor is running courses at the Central Hospital to train Solomon Island nurses, under the auspices of the World Health Organisation.

A malaria eradication pilot project began in October 1961 with the arrival of the W.H.O. project leader, and intensive training of local staff as technicians and squad leaders. Following a geographical reconnaissance of the selected area, Guadalcanal and New Georgia, spraying operation began in October 1962 and the fourth and final round of spraying was begun in June 1964. The results of the pilot project have been very encouraging and a Pre-eradication Project will begin in 1965.

The expenditure of the Medical Department in 1963 was £A214,726.

Communications

The aerodromes used by international air services are at Honiara (Henderson Field) on Guadalcanal, Yandina in the Russell Islands and Munda on New Georgia. In addition there are domestic aerodromes at Barakoma, Vella Lavella, Auki (Gwaanaru'u) on Malaita. Aerodromes under construction are at Kira Kira in San Cristobal, Sege in New Georgia and Mono (Stirling) in the Treasury Islands.

International air services are provided by a weekly service between Honiara and Lae, New Guinea, operated by Trans Australia Airlines, on behalf of Qantas Empire Airways, and by a twice weekly service between Suva, Fiji and Honiara by way of Santo and Vila in the New Hebrides and Nadi in Fiji. Domestic air services are operated by a local airline Megapode Airways and, between Munda, Yandina and Honiara, by Trans Australia Airlines as part of its international route.

The Posts and Telecommunications Department provides the following public commercial services:

(i) Postal air and surface letter, packet and parcel service to all parts of the world and within the Protectorate;

(ii) Direct telegraph and radiotelephone links between Honiara—Sydney, Honiara—Vila and Honiara—Suva and, through these centres and the Compac cable, communications with most countries of the world;

(iii) Direct telegraph communication with Tarawa in the Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony;

(iv) Internal telegraph and radio-telephone communications with the District stations and, through these, with other centres; and

(v) Telegraph communication with ships at sea, through the Honiara coastal station.

The department also operates and maintains the aeronautical telecommunications services and a shipping guard for local and foreign going ships.

There are 66 miles of motorable road with a rolled coral surface and permanent bridges on Guadalcanal and several miles of motorable tracks which are all being extended. There is a network of roads around the airfield at Munda on New Georgia and Barakoma on Vella Lavella. There are 3 miles of road on Gizo Island and 22 on Malaita which are being extended.

Messrs. Burns Philp and Company, Ltd., operate a freight and passenger service to the Protectorate via the New Hebrides from Sydney at intervals of approximately six weeks; the Australia-New Guinea Line operates a similar service direct from Australian ports. The Australian West Pacific Line and the China Navigation Company operate a freighter service between Hong Kong and Sydney calling at Honiara quarterly on the southward journey only. The Rotterdam Lloyd Line and the Bank Line also operate regular monthly services from the United Kingdom and the Continent. Bank Line vessels also call at intervals of six weeks to load copra. Since 1962 petroleum products have been shipped in large tankers and stored at Honiara.

The chief ports in the Protectorate are Honiara at which a deep water berth is in course of construction, Yandina and Gizo. The Government owns and operates 28 small vessels for inter-island
communications and administrative and medical touring, and one small fishing vessel. There is a considerable number of privately owned vessels in the Protectorate.

Broadcasting

The Solomon Islands Broadcasting Service (callsign VQO and VQO-3) transmits daily from 07.30 to 11.15 hours GMT on weekdays and from 09.00 to 11.15 hours GMT on Saturdays and Sundays. There is a half-hour broadcast for schools each Monday morning and this service is being expanded.

Principal Occupations

Most of the indigenous population is engaged in agriculture, either in village gardens or in the copra industry. They are under no economic necessity to work for wages. In 1963 the total number employed was estimated at 8,000 of whom 2,914 were employed on agricultural estates.

The first trade union in the Protectorate, the British Solomon Islands Workers' Union, was registered in 1961, and has approximately 4,000 members; a second, registered in 1963, has 400.

Main Crops and Products

The principal crop grown for export is coconuts; cocoa is also developing being planted by both Solomon Islanders and Europeans. Crops grown for local consumption include yams, sweet potatoes, taro and other roots, canarium almonds, cassava, rice, groundnuts, maize, bananas, pineapples and market garden vegetables.

A rough estimate of the livestock population is 10,000 pigs, 4,000 cattle, 300 goats and 100 horses.

Large scale timber operations have recently commenced and four companies are actively engaged in the extraction of hard wood logs principally for export from the country's large stands of exploitable timber. The Government acquired 180,000 acres of timber in 1964 when the production of logs amounted to 20 million super feet. Production will increase rapidly in future years and a sawmilling industry is also envisaged.

Marketing and Co-operative Organizations

The British Solomon Islands Protectorate Copra Board, which has agents at the main ports, purchases all copra from producers and sells it overseas. A small staff exists under a Registrar of Co-operative Societies to introduce the Solomon Islander to co-operative methods for economic development. The first co-operative society was formed in 1957 and there are now 60 primary societies and two secondary organizations. Most are at present concerned with the marketing of copra and the distribution of consumer goods.

THE CENTRAL AND SOUTHERN LINE ISLANDS

These consist of Flint, Caroline, Vostock, Malden and Starbuck, and are administered direct by the High Commissioner for the Western Pacific, formerly under Article 6 of the Pacific Order in Council, 1893, and more recently in accordance with instructions issued by the Secretary of State in 1903, under Article 4 of the Order in Council.

Flint Island was discovered in 1801 but continued search has failed to elicit the name or nationality of the discoverer. Since 1872 it has been worked for guano and copra, with interruptions, by various British companies under licence issued by the High Commissioner.

Caroline Island was discovered in 1705 by Captain W. R. Broughton of H.M. Sloop Providence and it has been worked by various British companies since 1846.

Vostock Island was discovered by Captain Bellingshausen, the Russian explorer, during his expedition to the South Pacific and Antarctic in 1820 and has been worked sporadically by British companies for guano and copra since 1873. It is now unoccupied and unworked.
Malden Island was discovered by Lord Byron, Captain of H.M.S. Blonde, in 1825, after conveying the remains of the King and Queen of the Sandwich Islands back to Honolulu from England for burial. Its guano deposits were worked by an Australian company from approximately 1860 to 1927. It is now unoccupied and unworked.

Starbuck Island was discovered in 1823 by Captain Starbuck, of the whaler L'Aigle owned by a London company. It was first worked for guano by a British company during the eighteen-sixties. Attempts to plant coconuts on the island failed and since 1920 it has been unoccupied and unworked.
