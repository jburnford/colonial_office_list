GAMBIA OFFICE
147–149 Grand Buildings, Trafalgar Square, W.C.2.
Telephone: WHItehall 1911/2.
Commissioner—L. F. Valentine, C.B.E.
A Joint Office was established by the Sierra Leone and Gambia Governments in 1954, its main functions being: to foster trade with Sierra Leone and the Gambia; to assist visitors from those territories; to undertake publicity and information services in the United Kingdom; to assist in the recruitment of staff for government and quasi-government posts in Sierra Leone and the Gambia; and to undertake general supervision of students in the United Kingdom. In accordance with the decision of the Sierra Leone Government, the Gambia Office has been separated from the Office of the High Commissioner for Sierra Leone with effect from the 27th April 1961. The Commissioner for the Gambia is now responsible for the control of all Gambian affairs which were previously dealt with by the Commissioner of the combined office.

MAURITIUS COMMISSION
153/156 Grand Buildings, Trafalgar Square, W.C.2.
Telephone: TRAfalgar 1080.
Cables: Maurcom, London, W.C.2.
Commissioner—Dr. L. Teelock.
Secretary—R. Chasle.
The Mauritius Commission was established on the 12th May 1964. The main functions of the Commission are the promotion of trade, industry and tourism; the dissemination of general information on Mauritius; the exploration of the U.K. employment market possibilities with a view to assisting Mauritians to emigrate; and the welfare generally of Mauritians visiting or residing in the U.K. The Commission exercises an oversight on the Mauritius Students Unit which is a branch of the Mauritius Ministry of Education. The Commission also works in close collaboration with the Colonial Office, the Ministry of Overseas Development and the Crown Agents for Oversea Governments and Administrations, and keeps in touch with other institutions and organisations, the role of which is to promote the development of the Commonwealth and the welfare of its peoples.

MAURITIUS HOUSE
16 Upper Montagu Street, W.1.
Telephone: AMBassador 2481.
Acting Secretary—J. M. Drummond.
Mauritius House was founded during the second world war by Viscountess Kemsley for the
benefit of Mauritians serving in the Forces. It now carries out welfare work, particularly among students, and acts as an information centre for the Colony.

Mauritius House is financed by the Government of Mauritius but is controlled by a private company, which until January 1951 operated under the War Charities Act and is now incorporated under the Companies Act, 1948.
